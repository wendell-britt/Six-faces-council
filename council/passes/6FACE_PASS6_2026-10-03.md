## 🌊 Shaman, from the Emotional Body's research

**Co-creative tools keep the designer's ownership; fully automated ones lose it.** Interviews with game
designers found that interactive back-and-forth preserved agency and a sense of ownership, and full
automation lost both ([Game Plot Design with an LLM-powered Assistant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02714)).
So a daemon proposes, and a face and Wendell decide.

**One finding pushes back on the daemons' Open Up.** Wendell's own brainstorm flow says *"The player
authors and chooses everything; the system never generates an idea"* (bars-engine `BrainstormFlow.tsx`).
That rule is about the player's brainstorm inside the game. A daemon's Idea Storm generates options for a
face, never for Wendell's own play, and the line between them stays where his flow draws it.

## ⚔️ Challenger, from the Skeptic's research

**Multi-agent pipelines help on work that splits into independent parts, and lose on work where one agent
feeds the next.** The Skeptic cites a failure taxonomy (the MAST study) in which most failures sit in the
handoffs between agents, not in any one agent. It gave no links, so these claims are unverified here.

**The daemon design already avoids the chain.** A face sends daemons on independent problems and reads
their reports itself; no daemon hands work to another. That rule is now written into each agent.

## 🏛 Regent, from the Protector's research

**Current practice tiers approval by consequence:** act without asking on reversible, low-impact work;
notify and proceed on recoverable work; block for approval on irreversible or outward work
([FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/ai-agent-compliance-governance-2026);
[capability-based tooling](https://medium.com/@2nick2patel2/llm-backends-need-permissions-not-prompts-capability-based-tooling-sandboxing-and-audit-trails-06426c9a9e7b)).

**The council already has those three tiers.** A daemon acts within read-only tools. A position stands
unless Wendell flips it, which is the notify tier. A question on the reserved list blocks until he answers.
Daemons keep read-only tools and web access, and never write.

## 🧠 Architect, from the Controller's research

**Small models for focused subtasks, and caching for content that does not change, are the two biggest
savings in the sources found** ([prompt caching](https://usewire.io/blog/how-prompt-caching-cuts-ai-agent-costs/)).
The daemon definition file is the same on every call, so it is the input worth caching.

**The cost has to be measured, not assumed.** Each daemon in this pass used between 64,000 and 73,000
tokens, most of it reading. A pass record now carries the subagent token count, so the scheme's saving can
be shown against a face doing the same work itself.

## 🎭 Diplomat, from the Fixer's and the Victim's research

**Agents are poor at deciding when to ask.** On HiL-Bench, pass rates fall sharply when an agent has to
judge whether to escalate ([HiL-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09408);
[Self-Gated Clarification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11349)). A question earns its place when its answer
changes a decision. Each daemon question now names the decision it would change, so the face can run the
reach test on it in one step.

**The cost of agent work falls on the person who reviews it.** Engineers disengage from long agent output
([I'm Not Reading All of That](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14225)), and review queues grow
([Faros](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-code-quality-senior-engineer-review-burden)). Wendell reviews on a
phone. So daemon reports stay short, and only the face's use of them reaches him.

## 📖 Sage, from the Damaged Self's research

**A model is a poor judge of its own output.** Self-correction has a measurable blind spot
([Self-Correction Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02778)), and a separate critic catches more
([LLM Critics Help Catch LLM Bugs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215)). That is why this pass checked the
daemons' citations with a separate checker on a larger model, and why the face, not the daemon, judges a
report.

**What each face gave.** The Shaman kept the designer's ownership and found the brainstorm boundary. The
Challenger kept daemons off chains. The Regent mapped the council's tiers onto current practice. The
Architect named caching and asked for the cost to be measured. The Diplomat tied every question to a
decision and kept reports short. The Sage kept judging outside the daemon.

**There was no dissent.** None of the faces disagreed on the fine-tunes. That unanimity is recorded as a flag, as the
rule requires. The live split is the model question below, between the Architect's saving and the Regent's
reliability.

## The one question for Wendell

**The smallest model failed the citation test on instruction-following, not on invented sources. How
should the daemons run?**

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Keep the smallest model, add the check and one retry** | The cheapest path. A report that fails the shape or link check goes back to its daemon once. The test reruns on the next research pass. |
| **Move to the middle model** | The fix set before the test ran. It costs about three times as much per token, and the test reruns. |

**Why only he can answer:** it trades his money against reliability, and the scheme is his. **Why it was not
asked before:** the test ran tonight.
