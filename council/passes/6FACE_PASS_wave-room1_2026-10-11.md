## Anchor

The room is the home. A charge is welcomed in through a door, felt, named, granted its right to be there, and then
either kept and aimed at what the player wants or let go on an out-breath. Whatever still claims to belong starts the
cycle again. The cave is where the player explores; the room is where what turns up gets worked.

## What the record already answers

**The steps already exist in the game.** `WAVE_STEPS` holds his sequence word for word: Welcome, then Acknowledge,
Allow, Accept and Appreciate, then Validate ("Your body has the right to feel this, whatever it is"), then Exhale, which
already asks the alignment question. The room stages a flow the ontology game has run since 7 October. The book's WAVE
stays as written (`oag-book-untouched`).

**Acknowledge is naming.** His note gives Acknowledge "a name and an element". The WAVE battle ruled the same way
(`wave-naming`: "the acknowledge phase would be where the spirit would get a name"), and the cave's pool already offers
the five elements with "I don't know".

**A charge that starts as a shadow and shows itself is ruled.** `wave-reveal`: a charge starts as a grey shadow and the
first Welcome shows its feeling, which is its element. In the room the door is where that happens.

**The player writes every word.** `cave-321-player-writes` stands, and so does the cave's rule that written words stay
on the page and are not saved with the scan.

**The 3-2-1 is built once.** `cave-321-one-build` and his answer `cave-321-form` = staged. The room's "stays" branch is
that same 3-2-1, aimed at the goal.

**The elements map to the alchemy moves.** The ontology names a Transcend move for each element (Wood: Commit to

Translate moves between them. The extension he names has its table already.

## Shaman

**Gathering, changing to Fullness:** "You are in a room and you may have forgotten why you came." The hexagram is this
design: a room where what is scattered collects around a centre, at a price. The changing lines warn that a full room
starts to wane, and the reading bears on the exit: what leaves should leave because the room is full of it, not because
the player wants it gone.

**The Shaman fears one phrase: "show it the exit".** From the Emotional Body daemon: "That wording makes the player the
bouncer." The player has just validated the feeling's right to be there; a push at the door undoes it. The daemon cites
Focusing's clearing a space, "acknowledge it briefly, name it if necessary, say hello maybe, and then put it to one
side" (its quote), and IFS's "Parts will only unburden when they're ready" (its quote). Set down, not thrown out.

**The Shaman makes leaving the charge's own move.** From the same report: the door opens on the cave's out-breath, the
charge drifts to the threshold in its element's colour and crosses at its own pace, and nothing is pushed if the player
stays still. The seat never moves. After it goes, the door stays ajar and a small light in its colour stays on the
threshold, named by the player. It can come back.

**The Shaman reads a part that stays as proof, not failure.** A part that claims to belong is the sign the charge was
not ready. It comes back in as a visitor and gets its own Welcome.

**The Shaman's standing test passes:** a charge is something that arrived, not something wrong with the player.

## Architect

**Sunrise, changing to Passing Through:** "This is becoming visible faster than you planned." Most of the room is
already built in pieces: the cave's breath, figure, element pool and staged 3-2-1, and the game's step words. The
changing line warns of being a stranger judged fast, so the room reuses the cave's own pieces rather than arriving as
something new.

**The Architect lays out the room.** One small scene in the cave's folder, built from the cave kit: a seat facing a door,
the player's faceless figure on the seat, the cave's breath in the walls and light. The flow is a table of steps, like
the cave's `STRETCHES`, so it can change without new code:

| Step | What the player does | What the room does |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Says yes to a sensation that is here | The door opens on an out-breath and a grey shape comes in (`wave-reveal`) |
| Witness | Feels into it: where, and its texture (tightness, tension, numbness, strength, something else) | The shape takes the texture, as the cave's mouth does |
| Acknowledge | Writes a name for it and picks its element, or "I don't know" | It takes the element's colour and settles into the room |
| Allow, Accept | The two rungs, in the game's words | The room eases with each, as the cave's path does |
| Appreciate | Optional: "Find what it's been trying to do for you", or Not now | Not now moves on and is not a block |
| Validate | "Your body has the right to feel this, whatever it is" | The charge's light steadies |
| The fork | Answers where it is pushing the next move: toward what they want, or away | Two branches |
| Stays | The staged 3-2-1, aimed at the goal; ends with a next move the player writes | The charge takes a place in the room |
| Goes | Writes one line to it, if they want, and lets the out-breath carry it | The door opens on the exhale; it leaves at its own pace; a light stays on the threshold |
| What's left | "Is anything still here that says it belongs?" | Yes brings it to the door as a new charge, back to Welcome |

**The Architect ties it to the cave with a lantern.** A daemon or charge that turns up in the cave can be taken home:
the cave leaves a lantern at the exact spot (as a block's side passage does), the camera rises to the room, and the
charge comes through the door with its feeling already known (`wave-reveal`: "A charge from a block the player named
starts with its feeling known"). When the room is done, the lantern is the way back. No skip is lost, because the
charge is worked, only in another place.

**The Architect leaves two extensions for later, with their tables ready.** Daemons: when the player says what the
charge pushes them to do, the urge picks the daemon (`wave-spirit-from-charge`), and Acknowledge can offer its name.
Alchemy: a charge that stays can take its element's Transcend move as its next move; a charge that goes can leave by a
Translate move along the cycle. Neither is in the first slice.

## Challenger

**The Well, changing to Impasse:** "What you want is there and your reach is broken." The practice is sound; the
mechanics around it are where it fails. Impasse says the direct route is closed, so the Challenger names the joints
that break.

**The Challenger names the suppression trap.** From the Skeptic daemon: a goal-based verdict followed by "show it the
exit" is "a suppression instruction in costume". It cites a review in which acceptance-like strategies averaged d =
0.30 against suppression's d = 0.03, with "very high variability" between studies (its figures, not spot-checked). The
fix is the order he already gave: Validate rules on the feeling's right to be there, and the fork never does. The fork
asks where the charge pushes the next move, not whether the feeling is allowed.

**The Challenger names the flattering answer.** Nothing costs a player who calls every charge aligned. So "stays" ends
with one next move the player writes toward the goal. The easy answer is no longer the cheap one.

**The Challenger names the endless loop.** "Until the charge is fully metabolized" has no stop on a phone at midnight.
The Skeptic composts "fully" and the zero target: a 0 on a 0 to 10 scale invites a false zero. A round ends when the
player says it has settled, with the reading after shown beside the reading before. After three rounds in one sitting
(the council's placeholder, not his number) the room offers to rest the charge by the door with a lantern, so the next
visit starts there. Resting is not a skip: the charge is still in the room.

**The Challenger names the blocker a test resolves:** whether the exit reads as release or as pushing down. Until that
holds, the alchemy extension builds on sand. The test is in Tests below.

## Diplomat

**Banter:** "The exchange is already the whole of it." Two lakes feeding each other: the player and the charge trade,
and the trade is the game. The room should feel like hosting, not like a court.

**The Diplomat writes the room as a host would.** Each step's line says where the player is in plain words ("It's in
the room with you", "It's by the door"). The fork's two answers read as directions, not verdicts: "It's pushing me
toward what I want" and "It's pushing me away from it". The calm switch and "Step back" from the cave stay on screen.

**The Diplomat keeps the room for players outside the council.** A new player meets the room before the cave, so it is
the gentlest door into the practice. It needs no daemon names, no elements known and no model, and "I don't know" is
always an answer. The Damaged Self check-in (`cave-321-damaged-self-ask`) comes before the 3-2-1 when that daemon is
named.
