**The arithmetic, which the faces argue from.** Half of $100 is $50 a month for Kickstarter copies. At $5.60 a copy
plus an estimated $0.50 to $1.50 a copy to ship a batch to Wendell, $50 buys **7 or 8 copies a month**. The shipping
estimate is the council's, not KDP's, and the KDP cart shows the real figure. At 7 or 8 a month, 247 books take
**31 to 35 months**. If batch one goes out in November 2026, the last backer receives a book between mid and late
2029.

## Shaman (35 Sunrise, becoming 21 Bite)

Sunrise says this is becoming visible faster than planned, and Bite says nothing closes until it is said. Both point
at the backers. The paperback will be on Amazon, and signed copies will sell at $40, while most backers are still
waiting. A backer who finds that out before Wendell tells them feels passed over a second time, after a wait that
began in 2022.

The felt risk is not the pace. It is a pace that nobody named. A backer who knows the cadence and their place in it
can wait. A backer who sees strangers holding signed copies and hears nothing reads it as being forgotten.

**Verdict:** the batches are right. The cadence goes to every backer in plain words before batch one ships, and each
batch is announced on the day it is ordered.

## Architect (9 Restraint, becoming 29 Depths)

Restraint names a small rule that holds the whole: the $100 cap and its 50/50 split. Depths says the bottom has
another bottom. Under the cap sits the real constraint, which is that the Kickstarter half has no revenue of its own.
New-sale copies earn about $40 each, and Kickstarter copies earn nothing.

The machine works as follows. Each month, Wendell orders one batch of author copies with the month's $100. About half
go to backers, signed, with a pin where one is owed, and the $10.40 allowance covers each mailer. The other half
becomes signed stock for $40 sales. The 12 Gumroad preorders are new sales already paid for, so they ship from the
new-sales half and do not wait in the backer queue. Their $650 has already covered their copies.

There are two levers on pace, and the budget already holds both. The first is recycling part of each $40 sale into
the Kickstarter half. The second is sending Kickstarter copies straight from KDP, using the $10.40 allowance to pay
KDP's shipping instead of mailers. That reaches roughly 12 a month, but each box arrives unsigned and the pins need a
separate mailing. The council estimates KDP single-copy shipping at $4 to $5, and the cart gives the real number.

**Verdict:** a monthly batch, split by the budget's own rule, with Gumroad preorders in the new-sales half. Both pace
levers are a money choice, so they go to Wendell.

## Challenger (15 Understatement, becoming 53 Emergence)

Understatement warns that what is underplayed had better be there. "Rolling batches" undersells a three-year tail. At
7 or 8 books a month, the last of 247 backers waits until 2029. Emergence says there are stages and one is being
skipped. The skipped stage is telling backers the number before the first box ships.

Three findings would show this plan false. KDP author-copy shipping could cost far more than estimated, which batch one's
cart settles in one order. The new Kickstarter export could change 247, because pledges drop and some backers chose
digital since then. A month with no print budget stops the queue entirely, because the line is an experiment in a
family contribution that the family has to approve.

The Challenger dissents from keeping the budget's pace as the default. A promise kept at 7 a month for three years
asks more patience of backers than the book's own counter-con allows. The Challenger would put the recycle lever in
from batch one.

**Verdict:** ship batch one now and read the real landed cost from its cart. Tell backers the real cadence. Put the
pace to Wendell as a question.

## Regent (64 Almost, becoming 19 Spring)

Almost says the crossing looks finished and the last stride gets wet. The book is written, proofed, and Wendell called
the proof done on 2026-10-03, so the wet stride is fulfillment. Spring says the window has an edge. The edge is the
promise in the August update: the order is announced the day it is placed. Under batches that promise holds for each
batch.

The obligations are these. Backers are owed what their tier named, so tier text sets what goes in each mailer. A
backer's address is confirmed before their batch, not once for all, because a three-year queue outlives addresses. The
money follows the approved budget, and anything above it goes to the Volunteer CFO, as the spec requires. The order in
which named backers ship is a choice about people, and people are reserved to Wendell.

**Verdict:** the batches follow the budget. Each batch is announced the day it is ordered, and each backer's address is
confirmed two weeks before their batch. The backer order goes to Wendell.

## Diplomat (34 Vigour, becoming 1 Unbroken)

Vigour is force with no aim, and Unbroken is sending with nowhere for the return to land. The force here is the
launch: Amazon, signed copies and new readers. The open call to readers that Wendell chose for the first circle
(`gn-pilot`) also needs the book in public. The return that has nowhere to land is the backers' reply. If the only
channel is a one-way Kickstarter update, a backer cannot say "I moved" or "send mine digital-only".

The bridge is one short message to every backer before batch one. It says what is happening, their place in the queue,
the cadence, and a way to answer that confirms their address or releases their copy. Some backers will say they are
happy with the ebook. Every release shortens the queue for everyone else, and it must be offered as a free choice and
never as pressure.

The Diplomat dissents on one point. It would hold signed $40 sales until the first backer batch has landed, so no
stranger holds a signed copy before any backer does. The other faces read the 50/50 rule as Wendell's fairness answer
already given, and they let sales and batch one start together.

**Verdict:** one message to all backers with a reply path before batch one. Releases are welcomed and never asked
for.

## Sage (50 The Cauldron, becoming 57 Persuasion)

The Cauldron carries the work by its handles, and the handles are the budget's two rules: $100 a month, half to the
backers. Persuasion names an effect already happening and not yet admitted. That effect is the three-year tail. It is
built into the arithmetic, and nobody has said it to the backers.

Each face's contribution stays in the synthesis. The Shaman named the felt risk, which is a pace nobody says aloud.
The Architect drew the machine and found the Gumroad preorders' place in it. The Challenger did the arithmetic and set
the falsification. The Regent kept the August promise intact under batches. The Diplomat built the return path that
lets a backer answer.

Two dissents go to Wendell unresolved: the Challenger's wish to recycle sale revenue from batch one, and the Diplomat's
wish to hold signed sales until backers are served first. The first is folded into the pace question below. The
second stands as a position he can flip.

## Outputs

### Positions (stand unless Wendell flips them)

- `ship-batches-monthly`: one batch a month from the $100 print line, split per the budget. Reason: Wendell's ruling in
  chat plus E1. Nothing is left to his preference.
- `ship-gumroad-first`: the 12 Gumroad physical preorders ship from the new-sales half in batch one. Reason: the 50/50

  confirm an address or take ebook-only, and a promise to announce each batch on its day. Reason: E6 and the Shaman's

- `ship-address-per-batch`: addresses are confirmed two weeks before each backer's batch. Reason: a queue of up to 35
  months outlives a 2022 address. This is a logistics fact, not a preference.
- `ship-sales-alongside`: signed $40 sales start with batch one and are not held for backers. Reason: the 50/50 rule is
  Wendell's fairness answer. The Diplomat dissents.
- `ship-plan-superseded`: the one-shipment plan from the project thread is marked superseded, with a pointer here.
