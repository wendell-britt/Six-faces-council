# The podcast outreach move

A move the council runs whenever Wendell wants someone invited onto one of his podcasts. It turns a name into a
pitch that is about the guest: a topic chosen for what they know, tied to the book or to an episode already made,
and an invitation he can send as written. The contacts live on the Council Board's Outreach tab, and chases come
back on Your steps under Podcast chases (pass `outreach-on-board`, 2026-10-08).

## The ask, in his words

Wendell, 2026-10-09, in the Mastering the Game of Allyship project, asking for invitations to Marina and Dustin
Muñoz de Martínez, Adam (Masculine Witness), and the counselors he met at the Washington Mental Health Counselors
gala:

> I'd also like to extend the Podcast Outreach to have a step where we brainstorm topics for the people who are already on the list if we have access to information about them. If we DON'T we add a research step or a manual brainstorming so that our pitches are cusomized and add value to the people we are helping. We want the pitches to also be connected to topics from the book OR other episodes.

Its first run is that message: four invitations, and a topic pass over the open contacts on the list. Both are in
the MTGOA project files under `podcast/outreach/`.

## When it runs

It runs when Wendell names someone to invite, when he asks for pitches, and once over the list whenever a batch of
new contacts lands on the Outreach tab. It runs for any show he hosts. Tie-ins come from that show's own material:
the book and past episodes for Mastering the Game of Allyship, and each other show's own canon for the rest.

## The five steps

1. **Put the person on the list.** Every invitee gets a contact on the Outreach tab, with the show, a status of
   Identified, and a "why" in one or two sentences. A group invitation (the counselors from one event) gets one
   contact for the group.
2. **Check what is known.** The session reads the contact's row, any earlier thread about them, and anything
   Wendell said in his message. Enough is known when the session can say what the person does and what they would
   bring that the show doesn't already have. Then it goes to step 3.

   When not enough is known, the session researches first: their site, their writing, talks and podcasts they have
   been on, and their organization's page. What it finds goes into the contact's notes with the sources, and a
   claim it could not confirm is marked unconfirmed. When research finds nothing (a friend with no public profile, a
   first name only), the contact goes on the board as a question: what Wendell knows about them, with two or three
   guesses at a topic to react to. That is the manual brainstorm, and it is short.

   Some rows say to approach on the person's terms (Indigenous guests, for one). For those, the topic is not
   proposed. The pitch asks what they would want an episode to do.
3. **Brainstorm the topic.** The session writes two or three topics drawn from what the guest knows and recommends
   one. Each topic names what it ties to: a chapter of the book, a face, a move, or an episode already recorded or
   booked, with a chapter time when it is a past episode. A topic has to give the guest something: an audience for
   their work, a question they care about, a conversation they don't get to have elsewhere. When Wendell names the
   topic himself, the session keeps it and adds only the tie-ins. The recommended topic goes into the contact's
   notes as `Topic:` with the date, and stands until he changes it there.

   For a group, or when Wendell wants to find the topic with the guest, the invitation stays open and offers three
   or four starting points instead.
4. **Draft the invitation.** A subject line and an email in his voice: dry honesty over hype, no urgency, no
   flattery. It says how they know each other (a bracketed line when the session doesn't know), the topic in a
   sentence, why them, one tie-in to the book or an episode in plain words, what they get (the video and clips, a
   pointer to their work), and the booking link. It runs about two hundred words. Under it go two short notes for
   Wendell only: why the topic fits, and what it ties to. Drafts go in the project files at
   `podcast/outreach/invitations-<date>.md`.
5. **Hand it over.** Wendell sends every invitation himself. When he does, he marks the contact Sent on the board,
   and the Podcast chases list takes it from there.

## What it leaves alone

- It never sends, schedules or posts anything, and it never messages a guest. Writing to someone under his name is
  reserved to him.
- It writes nothing about a guest it could not source, and it keeps family and health details out of a pitch.
- A topic Wendell named is his. The move adds tie-ins and does not swap the topic.
- Client stories from counselors and coaches stay unidentifiable on air; the invitation says so when it applies.
