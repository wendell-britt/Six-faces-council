**The cast**
Hexagram 45 Gathering turns to 60 Measure. Gathering brings many parts into one room, and Measure asks where the edge is. I count the fight's parts and name its limit.

**The interview**
- My job here: test whether a first-time phone player can read the fight.
- How I would do it: list what the player meets, then check each item against what the screen teaches.
- Hours to the Player: the time of one fight, per charge.
- Pay to the Player: the charge leaves them, or a Witness Book page fills [1].
- Who I work for: the new player of the skilled-helper campaign.

**Wake Up**
- On screen: a strength word, a name, two HP bars, Mana, two stances, a weather line, seven buttons, a Magic panel with four moves and a stance menu, and a log [1].
- Off screen: a five-way element cycle, five feeling names, eight trigram names, a satisfy meter with need 8, a hold cap of two, level weights, speed and guard [1].
- Mana also buys hints at the hearth, so one pool serves both talk and fight [1].
- The fight never shows the wrong line. A charge keeps kind, element, person and beat, not the words [1].
- An Earth foe's plan reads "It means to bury you," but its gates are unanswerable, so the code strikes other weathers [1].
- A stance change gives the foe no turn [1].
- Each action has three names across the two files: Welcome, Validate, Exhale in wave.js; Listen, Hold, Let go in the template [2][1].
- The cost sentence, "Taking it costs HP and mana," appears on the talk screen, not in the fight [1].

About 25 concepts meet a first-time player, and the battle screen teaches few of them.

**Open Up (raw)**
A wrong-line quote; stance chips; the feeling on the weather line; a tutorial strike; fewer endings; cut Mana; satisfy as a bar; cut speed; drop the level word; a cycle diagram; Listen free; one name for each action.

**Clean Up**
Compost the tutorial strike (another fight before this one), the diagram (a second screen), and the Witness Book on the battle screen (it belongs to the book). Blockers: the Earth plan mismatch, the missing line, and four endings with one shown.

**Grow Up**
Test five phone players who have not seen the cycle. Ask them what Let go did and why a strike landed, and count fights finished without a retry. No outside reading needed; the problem sits in the code.

**Show Up**
1. Print the strike's feeling beside its weather, such as "Fire clings" with Anger. Cost: the Player answers a word, not a sense.
2. Move stance into five feeling chips above the buttons, since it costs no turn. Cost: one more row on a phone.
3. Store the wrong line's words on the charge and open the fight with them. Cost: each charge carries text.
4. Show two endings, Let go and Walk on, and drop the satisfy meter from the screen. Cost: spirits lose the stance-fed route, and the befriend count leaves the book.
5. Make the Earth foe say when your moves cannot answer it. Cost: Earth foes lose menace until wood moves arrive.

**For the Player**
It protects a first-time Player from spending HP and mana on guesses about a cycle they cannot see. It makes the fight enjoyable because it answers the exact line they got wrong.

**Your questions**
1. Is a stance change meant to cost no foe turn? The code gives none [1].
2. Is the befriend path held back on purpose from a first-time Player?

**Where you would overreach**
I would push the fight down to four moves and treat any rule the Player cannot master first time as a fault, when that depth may be part of the Player's power.
