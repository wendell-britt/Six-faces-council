## The cast
The Well says the want is already there and the reach to it is broken. For me that is the Controller's trap: I keep the want in the well and never draw it. Muster says the organising fact is one not yet said, so I build the approach around saying the want plainly.

- My job here: decide when the child is ready to meet me.
- How I would do it: a short check-in at the shut mouth, before the door opens, in the order body, emotion, veiled or flooded, satisfied or dissatisfied.
- Hours to the Player: a few minutes per approach, each question skippable.
- Pay to the Player: the child walks in knowing what the body is doing, with a spirit beside them.
- I work for: the Protector, the body's survival [2].

## Wake Up
The record has no approach check-in for any voice. The next chamber's mouth "shows no figure, only breathing light and a sound" [3]. A veiled spirit's state does not show: "the card reads 'can't see it yet'" [4]. So the check-in must ask about the veil before it asks about satisfaction, or the answer cannot be read. A wild spirit "is met dissatisfied" [4], so my family would arrive gripping too hard by default.

## Open Up
Ask body, emotion, veil, state. Gate the door on a satisfied reading. Send Holdfast spirits to fight the child. Make me a battle boss. Rehearse the approach until no reply can break it. Count a skipped question as a fault. Require the child to name the want before entering. Run a countdown before the door.

## Clean Up
Compost the gate, the fault count and the rehearsal. The record says "every question can be skipped" [5], and the spirits are meant to vouch, "like at the end of pokemon where you show the badges you've collected" [5], which is not a fight with me. The real blocker is order: the veil question must come before the state question.

## Grow Up
Test that the approach asks the veil before the state. Test that a "no word yet" answer opens the door. Read the family's honored form: "A satisfied Holdfast has let go: low and easy, feet set wide, head level and soft" [6].

## Show Up
1. Ask in order: body, emotion, veiled or flooded, then satisfied or dissatisfied. Each is skippable, and "no word yet" is an equal answer [5].
2. The door opens on passage, never on a satisfied reading.
3. My spirit is the Holdfast family, met dissatisfied as a grip held too hard. Its honored form is the let-go, walking beside the child [6].
4. I am the spirit that becomes me, not a trainer who sends spirits against the child.
5. Add a test that a veiled Holdfast shows no state until revealed [4].

## For the Player
This protects the child from meeting me cold, flooded, or without a word for what the body is doing. It makes the approach fun because the let-go is a visible reward, and the spirit the child brings vouches for them.
