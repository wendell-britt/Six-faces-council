## Wake Up
The strongest precedent shows that the player's own voice does the work. Slater's Freud study (58 people) compared a scripted Freud against alternating between your own body and Freud's. After a week, 86% of the self-conversation group reported changed thoughts or feelings, against 48% of the scripted group [1]. The authors caution that "most of the effects we found were not overwhelmingly supported in terms of probability" [1]. They also did not test unsupervised use [1]. So a figure that answers on the system's behalf is the weaker arm. Your rule that the system never writes a turn already matches the better condition.

## Clean Up
The failure to expect is the cutscene. If the figure answers in the "you" step and the player does not write the answer, the player watches instead of practising. Someone must author the daemon's lines, and that someone is the system, so the practice is broken. A scripted reply also cannot fit what this player said, which is the weak arm in [1]. Also, a new camera move costs build hours and adds nothing if the player types the same words. Compost the scripted answers and the voice recording. Keep the camera move only if the player authors the "you" lines themselves. Hellblade's voices work because the player's own sense-making builds the meaning. Fletcher says games start you "from scratch" and make you build expectations [3]. The voices were an advisor-led design, not a tested therapy, and I found no outcome data for it [3]. Slater's Freud study 2 had 22 people, no control group, and only mild problems [2]. Neither one tells us this encounter works. I found nothing for Celeste and did not read it, so it is unlinked.

## Grow Up
Run a within-player A/B on Wendell's phone, then on three or four players. One daemon is worked in the chat, and a similar one in the encounter, in a randomised order. Log four measures:
1. Charge drop (before minus after), which the chat already records.
2. Seconds spent.
3. The share of "I" turns written in first person, counted by a simple check.
4. The words typed in the "you" step.
The encounter has to win on charge drop, with typing no lower, to justify building. A drop with fewer words might mean the player is watching. A second look at one week later (did the daemon come back?) would copy [1], but is optional.
