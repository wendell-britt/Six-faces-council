## Wake Up
Your design already validates first (allow, accept), which is the right order. Allowing means "letting be" what you find [2], and parts "will only unburden when they're ready" and unburdening "can't be forced" [3]. The risk is the sentence "show it the exit." That wording makes the player the bouncer. Also, in the Shaman's altitude my distortion is settling the meaning early, so a rule like "not aligned means it leaves" decides before the charge has finished arriving.

## Open Up
Door opens wider on the out-breath. The charge walks out when ready. The player sets it on a threshold shelf first. The charge fades into its element (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water). The player asks "does it want to go?" in a 3-2-1 line. The seat turns toward the door. Distance grows only if the player holds. A light stays on the threshold. The door stays ajar after. Exit as a gift to the element. A rush of speed. A score for release. A tidy "gone" message.

## Clean Up
Blockers: (1) "show it the exit" implies expulsion; (2) "not aligned" is judged by the player's goals, which can make anger or fear feel unwelcome; (3) a part that claims to stay is the proof the charge was not ready, so it is not a failure; (4) any system-written closing line breaks your rule. Composted: scoring release, auto-fade timers, element lines written for the player.

## Grow Up
Focusing's clearing a space says to "acknowledge it briefly... say hello maybe, and then put it to one side," lightly, knowing you will return [1]. That is the model: set down, not thrown out. IFS releases into an element only when the part is ready [3]. RAIN warns that wishing a feeling would go away is itself resistance [2]. Test with Wendell: after one run, does he report "it left" or "I got rid of it"? Only the first counts.

## Show Up
1. Rename the step "let it go where it wants." The player writes one 3-2-1 line: "I am ready to let you go, and you can come back." The player invites; the charge chooses.
2. The door opens on the existing 6.5s out-breath. The charge drifts to the threshold in its element color, then crosses at its own pace. If the player stays still, nothing is pushed.
3. The seat never moves. The charge moves away and the distance grows by itself, then closes if called (clearing-a-space "return later" [1]). Nothing disappears from the room map.
4. After release, the door stays ajar and leaves a small lit mark in the element color on the threshold, named by the player. The charge left a trace the player chose, and the light stays lit (36).
5. A part that claims to stay reopens the cycle as a visitor with its own 3-2-1, never a rejection. The calm switch skips the animation but keeps the choice.

## For the Player
Protects Wendell from rehearsing suppression inside a game that said the feeling was welcome. Makes the exhale a relief, the door a courtesy, and the room a place the feeling can return to.
