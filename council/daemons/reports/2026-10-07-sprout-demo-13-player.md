**The cast:** Hexagram 61, Incubation, becomes 27, Feeding. Something is developing inwardly, hidden and preparing. The becoming asks what deserves nourishment and what should be withheld. The wisdom here is that not every option serves growth—selectivity in feeding is itself the cultivation.

**What I want here:** I want one clear move that works immediately. I want to see the spirit respond to what I did. I want to feel capable on the first try, not learn a system. I want to know what is actually possible right now, without discovering it through failure or text I have to read.

**What the superpower does with it:** The Connector says the true thing that is unspoken. The real problem is not the UI complexity. The real problem is asking a new player to learn emotional alchemy, five elements, and eight trigrams before they know what the game is. The Connector names what the player actually wants: "Let me do one thing and see it work. Then tell me what it means after I trust the system." The superpower stops defending the eight-button screen and says what feeding actually means: nourish one action until it grows into understanding, not the other way around.

**What FF4 to FF6 ask and hold back:** The side-view shows a person facing a monster, which matches my understanding. The command menu shows Fight, Magic, Item, Run. In the opening, the player character often starts with limited options. Fight is always available. When other party members arrive, they bring their own abilities—Rosa brings White Magic, Rydia brings Black Magic. The player's first battles ask one question: "Can you press Fight?" That feedback teaches the entire lesson. Attack never fails, never has no effect, never requires understanding a system. Magic and Items arrive as the player grows comfortable, learned from story and gathering, not from reading a screen.

**What I would cut or hide first:** The trigram buttons beyond the four straight gates. Then the hold-and-release twitching. Then I would hide the Witness Book. Then I would make the stances optional on the first strike. What I would keep: Welcome (the Fight button—it always works), one answer (the simplest one), see the spirit respond, feel the push, try again. One input per beat.

**What would make it fun:** Give me the same feedback FF gives. I press Welcome, the spirit does something, I see what happened, then I get to do it again. Every success is visible. Every choice has an effect. I am not reading buttons; I am reading the spirit's response to what I did.

**Show Up:** The immediate move is to test what happens if the first spirit only accepts Welcome (all other buttons disabled). Does the player learn what listening does? Does the player feel ready for the next input after one success?

**My question:** Should the first real spirit the player faces after the taught fight only respond to Welcome, with all other buttons disabled, so the player learns what one action does before choosing between five stances and eight gates?
