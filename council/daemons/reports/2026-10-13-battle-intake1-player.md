**The cast (Diplomat, hexagram 36 Nightfall, line 2 changing to 11 Mingling).** The reading says a light is kept covered and is still lit. The wrong line is that light: it was hidden under a talk that moved on. Mingling says the fight should bring the two halves together, not discharge the charge to the ground.

**What I want here.** I want the spirit to be the same person I was when I said the wrong line, so I can answer it the way I should have in the talk, and be heard for it.

**What the current fight does.** The spirit's element is the need of the person last spoken to (prototype.template.html ~line 331). Its gate comes from an element plan (~lines 315-319, wave.js ~47-51). The wrong line appears once, as a scene line on the first beat (~line 450). The answer is a trigram element, picked from eight names at 2 mana each. Hold and Let go is a two-state seize. Listen spends a turn. Fight only subdues. A wrong answer costs HP on top of the talk's cost. The talk's mistake plays no part.

**What the superpower does with it.** The Diplomat's superpower is the Connector: the true thing gets said without anybody leaving, and the connection that results has survived being told the truth. Applied here, the fight lets the Player say the want at the table, with the spirit still present to answer. For a newcomer, that means the fight is a second chance at the same conversation, not a penalty for the first one.

**Show Up.** The five moves, distilled.
1. The spirit opens by saying its wrong line back, close to word for word. The field `pushText.dissatisfied` already exists, so this is a small change.
2. The right answer is the helping skill that was missed, such as reflecting the feeling the talk hurried past. It shows as three cards, one tap each. The eight trigram names move to the Witness Book as a lesson, or stay as the spirit's colour.
3. The spirit's next line shows in words before the Player chooses. Slay the Spire shows enemy intent above the enemy so players can plan around it [4]. This removes the guessing that Listen currently buys.
4. Three turns at most, and the fight ends when the spirit has said what it needed. Undertale's ACT works this way: monsters hint in speech, and a peaceful ending follows from acting on the hint [5][6]. Fight stays a path, but it only subdues.
5. A wrong card makes the spirit repeat its line louder, with no second HP tax. The Witness Book then records the skill that would have landed. Griftlands and Persona use negotiation where the goal is to win the other side's agreement, not to deal damage. Pokémon's type chart and Disco Elysium's inner voices are the other reference points. I did not check Griftlands, Persona, Pokémon or Disco Elysium against a source, so those points come from memory and carry no link.

**What I protect the Player from.** Losing HP for a mistake already paid for in the talk. Dead turns spent on Listen. A menu of eight names to memorise on a phone. A verdict that the Player was simply wrong.

**What would make it fun.** The spirit says its line back, the Player taps a skill card, and the spirit says what it needed. A fight of about ninety seconds, and it ends on a spoken want.

**Where I would overreach.** If the skill cards become a quiz with one right answer, the battle stops being Egan's model, which allows more than one good line. The five-element teaching is also the game's stated purpose, so replacing the element answer key could remove it. The safer route keeps element as the spirit's colour and lets the skill carry the answer. The numbers I cite are placeholders from the existing code. Nothing here changes rules, balance or story without a ruling from Wendell.

**My questions.**
1. Should the answer to a spirit be the helping skill, with element as its colour, or should the five-element answer key stay? This decides whether the fight teaches Egan or the five elements. It needs a ruling.
2. Should a wrong card still cost HP, or should the talk's mistake be the only cost? This sets the stake on a phone.
