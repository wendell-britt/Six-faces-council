## The cast
Hexagram 42, Windfall: more arrives than the player put in, and a duty attaches to the gain. A stranger gives a few honest taps and the game returns a whole creature. The duty is to give back something true, and not to claim more insight than the taps carried.

## The interview
- My job here: be where the charge is received, so the spirit is built from what is moving now.
- How I would do it: ask four small things (a feeling, where in the body, how strong, one thought). The player's own word becomes the spirit's name.
- Hours it costs the Player: about 20 to 40 seconds for the first charge, near zero after, since saved answers can prefill.
- Pay the Player gets: a creature that is recognizably theirs, and a name they authored.
- Who I work for: the Diplomat face, on behalf of the phone stranger.

## Wake Up
- The game already has the feeling input. Prompted and integrated modes ask which of five feelings is moving, and the charge takes that element (task brief).
- The game already holds the thought. The intake has six picked beliefs, each with a daemon and an element, for example "I'm not ready" with Protector and Metal. Picks "stay on the device" (`/home/user/sprout/data/intake.json`).
- So the missing inputs are body location and intensity, plus the naming act. design.md has no "Acknowledge" entry I could find, so naming is probably thin in the data.
- Research supports each input. Labeling "diminished the response of the amygdala" [1]. Body maps are "statistically separable" per emotion across 701 participants [2]. The Mood Meter pairs pleasantness with energy, which is intensity [3]. How We Feel starts with a check-in, then tags causes [4]. Granularity, the precision of emotion-making, is linked to better coping [5].
- Games already do this. Sea of Solitude's monstrous look "serves as a mirror of their emotions" [6]. Badeline personifies the hero's anxiety, and Madeline learns to "function with it" instead of beating it [7]. Nemesis names an enemy from what happened in play [8].

## Open Up
- Body silhouette tap, then the spot sets the creature's anatomy or aura.
- Intensity slider sets size, speed, or HP.
- Belief pick sets the daemon type and moveset.
- Feeling element sets palette and move element.
- The player types or chooses a name, and the name is the spirit.
- Offer name suggestions from a granular word list (irritated, resentful, wary).
- Infer from play: hesitation, tap pressure, retries, time of day.
- Mood Meter grid in place of five buttons.
- Daily check-in that carries over.
- Spirit remembers the last fight, Nemesis style.
- Free text parsed by a model.
- "Skip" that rolls random.
- Spirit speaks the player's own phrase back.

## Clean Up
- Compost free text parsed by a model. It risks misreading the player, and it sends private words off the device.
- Compost inference from behaviour as the main source. Silent diagnosis breaks consent, and it can be wrong. Allow it only as a gentle default the player can overrule.
- Compost the full Mood Meter grid. Its 100 words are heavy on a phone.
- Compost the daily check-in that carries over. It implies ongoing monitoring.
- Block: five elements are coarser than the research vocabularies. Anger, sadness and fear are in the map, but "neutrality" is a stretch. The game maps "five feelings" to elements, yet Barrett's framework resists basic emotions [5]. Treat the element as the game's lens, not a diagnosis.
- Block: therapy-cosplay. Copy the work of the mechanics, and skip claims about outcomes.

## Grow Up
- Playtest whether players type a name or pick one. Typing is more authored, picking is less exposed.
- Measure the skip rate at each input. A high skip rate on body location means it is too intimate.
- Read Lieberman 2007 for what labeling needs. The effect came from words on emotional images [1], so a chosen word may matter more than a typed sentence.
- Check Undertale and Persona handling in a second pass, which I did not reach.

## Show Up
1. Ask in this order: feeling (existing), body spot on a silhouette, intensity as a three-step choice, then the thought as a pick from the player's own six beliefs. Make every step skippable, and let a skip fall back to random for that field.
2. Make Acknowledge the generator. The player picks or types a name, offered with three granular synonyms for their feeling. The spirit's displayed name is that word, and the player's word returns in its lines.
3. Build the creature from fixed parts. Element sets palette and move element, body spot sets the anatomy or aura, intensity sets size, the belief's daemon sets the moveset. Nothing is generated beyond these parts, so a misread cannot happen.
4. Stay in the fiction. The spirit says "something here feels like..." and never states a diagnosis. Keep all answers on the device, and show no summary of the player.
5. Let the player re-name or re-cast mid-fight ("not quite it"), so a wrong guess costs one tap.

## For the Player
Protects the phone stranger from being profiled, diagnosed, or pushed to disclose. Make it more enjoyable by making the foe feel authored by them, with a name that is theirs and a fight that answers it.
