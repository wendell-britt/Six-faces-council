# Design Ontology Seed — Six Game Master Council Brief

**Status:** Provisional ontology seed for council ingestion and cross-environment translation
**Owner:** Wendell Britt
**Primary use:** Shared context for a six-face strand/council run
**Suggested order:** Shaman → Architect → Challenger → Regent → Diplomat → Sage

## Council mandate

Teach every participating agent the design ontology below, then use the six Game Master faces to translate it faithfully through their respective environments.

This is not a request to praise or merely summarize the ontology. Each face must:

1. demonstrate accurate comprehension before evaluation;
2. reason from its assigned lens without replacing the source ontology with that lens;
3. translate concepts into its environment while preserving their source meaning and provenance;
4. identify tensions, missing distinctions, possible equivalences, and losses in translation;
5. distinguish constitutional principles from provisional hypotheses;
6. preserve productive disagreement and ambiguity rather than forcing consensus;
7. record candidate changes without ratifying, pruning, merging, or composting them during this pass.

The intended output is a **faithful six-face ingestion record**, a cross-environment translation map, unresolved questions, and a readiness assessment for a later governance pass.

This first council run is **Phase A: Ingestion and Translation**. It must not decide the smallest coherent ontology, remove distinctions, or assign concepts to active/reference/compost sets. Those decisions belong to **Phase B: Operational Synthesis and Governance**, after all faces can accurately restate and apply what they have ingested.

## 1. Purpose

The design ontology is an executable design philosophy for games, game-design tools, AI collaborators, and Game Master agents.

It should help an AI:

- recognize what Wendell finds meaningful and enjoyable;
- generate surprising ideas inside a recognizable aesthetic field;
- distinguish resonant invention from arbitrary novelty;
- translate personal aesthetics into expected player experiences;
- identify the psychographic assumptions embedded in a design;
- create later versions for wider audiences without erasing the originating pleasure;
- expose design shadows and turn them into playable remediation;
- know when an idea should be developed, merged, deferred, handed off, or composted.

The ontology is not intended to eliminate hallucination. It should create a meaningful possibility space in which AI speculation remains related to the designer's values, aesthetics, and methods of judgment.

## 2. Constitutional principles

> **Version-lineage note (decided 2026-10-10):** The eight principles below are the preserved constitutional seed. After aesthetic excavation, cross-specimen extraction, and framework translation, they were amended, expanded, and restated as the fifteen-principle provisional v0.1 constitution in `design-philosophy-draft-v0.1.md`. All eight lineages continue there; seven principles were added. For current unqualified constitutional retrieval, use the fifteen. Preserve this section as the provenance-bearing seed. See `council-phase-a/constitutional-evolution/council-decision.md`.

### P1. The Game Master is also a player

The Game Master must receive uncertainty, discovery, agency, meaningful choice, feedback, and enjoyment from the system. A game that requires the Game Master to function only as an exhausted service provider has failed.

### P2. The game designer is also a player

Designing should itself contain play: experimentation, surprise, interpretation, transformation, and consequential choice. The designer belongs inside the field of experience rather than standing wholly outside it.

### P3. Design first for the designer's psychographic neighborhood

The first hypothesis is:

> If the game is fun for me, it may be fun for people sufficiently like me.

This is a hypothesis to test, not an assumption to universalize.

Use concentric drafts:

1. **Draft 0 — Designer as player:** Find the authentic pleasure.
2. **Draft 1 — Psychographic near-neighbors:** Test which pleasures transfer.
3. **Draft 2 — Adjacent configurations:** Add alternate affordances and explanations.
4. **Draft 3 — Wider translations:** Increase accessibility and reach while protecting the core loop.

Later drafts translate the work; they must not retroactively sterilize the originating game.

### P4. The body gets a vote

Conceptual coherence is provisional until it meets lived and somatic experience. The body is a source of first-person evidence, not merely an object represented by the ontology.

Designs should ask:

- Where is this experienced in the body?
- What is its texture, intensity, location, movement, or absence?
- What changes when a belief, rule, or intervention is held as true?
- Did something genuinely shift, remain the same, or become less clear?

A lack of change is information, not failure.

### P5. The helper belongs inside the field of care

No helping system is successful if it consumes, erases, or permanently obligates the helper. Capability, integrity, benevolence, boundaries, replenishment, and enjoyment all belong inside the design of helping.

### P6. The tool practices what it teaches

A design tool should remediate the conditions that make the tool necessary. If the ontology identifies a recurring design shadow, the design process should include a playable practice for meeting that shadow.

### P7. Composting is a valid completion

A possibility can be honored, metabolized, and released without being implemented. Good-enough completion, deliberate abandonment, merging, delegation, and composting are legitimate design outcomes.

### P8. Motivation does not substitute for meaning

Increased engagement is not automatically evidence of fun, value, or transformation. Motivational techniques must support the intended aesthetic experience, player agency, Emotional Alchemy movement, and ethical commitments.

## 3. Framework stack and boundaries

| Framework | Question | Function | Must not become |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Designer–Player Continuity** | Who must find this intrinsically playable? | Establishes the originating aesthetic and first audience | An excuse to ignore evidence from other players |
| **AQAL** | Which perspectives, lines, levels, states, and types are involved? | Coverage, psychographic mapping, anti-reductionism | A totalizing answer or a ranking of whole people |
| **Emotional Alchemy** | How does charge become awareness, capacity, action, or artifact? | Transformation grammar and energy economy | A morality wheel or demand that every feeling be “fixed” |
| **Octalysis** | What motivates participation, and at what cost? | Motivation audit and engagement vocabulary | A checklist or justification for compulsion |
| **Somatic verification** | What is actually felt, and what changes? | First-person evidence and corrective to intellectualization | A claim that bodily response has only one interpretation |
| **BAR lifecycle** | What happens to captured possibility? | Provenance, stewardship, action, release, compost | An infinite backlog of unresolved obligations |

## 4. AQAL design contract

Every important design claim should be inspectable through all four quadrants.

| Quadrant | Designer side | Player side | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UL — I** | Intention, intuition, felt judgment, aesthetic attraction, shadow | Emotion, attention, interpretation, identity, felt meaning | Interviews, journals, phenomenological and somatic reports |
| **UR — It** | Craft behavior, prototypes, demonstrated capability | Choices, actions, learning, bodily behavior, completion | Observation, playtests, telemetry, artifacts |
| **LL — We** | Influences, values, creative culture, ethical commitments | Shared meaning, norms, relationships, community interpretation | Group dialogue, discourse, ethnography |
| **LR — Its** | Tools, production systems, team structure, economics | Game economy, governance, matchmaking, access infrastructure | System analysis and longitudinal effects |

Quadrants are perspectives on the same event, not four unrelated content buckets.

For each feature or pattern, identify:

- relevant developmental **lines**;
- the **level of complexity** demanded within each line;
- temporary **states** the design may invite;
- non-hierarchical **types** it favors or excludes;
- the evidence required to distinguish intended experience from actual experience.

Do not assign a single developmental level to an entire person. Do not treat type as level.

## 6. Intended player transformation: the skilled helper

The central intended audience includes people who want to become skilled helpers and existing helpers who want to help others and themselves more effectively.

The desired movement is:

> I feel responsible → I recognize that I genuinely want to help → I distinguish desire from guilt → I determine what help is actually needed → I develop or locate the relevant capacity → I act without erasing myself → I observe what changed → I integrate the result.

The design must distinguish:

- wanting to be good from being useful;
- benevolent intention from capability;
- action from effective action;
- sacrifice from sustainable contribution;
- rescuing from increasing another person's agency;
- offering help from obligating oneself indefinitely.

Use the trust triad:

- **Capability:** Can I or they do this?
- **Integrity:** Will I or they follow through?
- **Benevolence:** Is shared interest genuinely represented?

Trust is a structural relationship assessment, not a single emotion. Any trust condition may be emotionally inflected through the Emotional Alchemy channels.

## 7. Emotional Alchemy contract

Emotional Alchemy is an **energy economy, not a morality wheel**.

### Five channels

| Element | Channel | Signal / lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Metal | Fear | Risk or opportunity detected; discernment and clarity |
| Water | Sadness | Something valued is distant, absent, or misaligned |
| Wood | Joy | Vitality, growth, connection, or possibility detected |
| Fire | Anger | An obstacle or boundary violation is present |
| Earth | Neutrality | Whole-system perspective, stability, or witnessed detachment |

### Within-channel progression

1. **Wake:** Notice the signal.
2. **Clean:** Correct distortion and clarify what is present.
3. **Grow:** Extract the lesson or develop capacity.
4. **Show:** Act in alignment with the lesson.

These four stages are not called WAVE. The broader personal-practice set is the five **Basic Moves**: Wake Up, Open Up, Clean Up, Grow Up, and Show Up. **WAVE** is reserved for the breathing practice associated with Open Up. The Basic Moves, the within-channel progression, and the canonical fifteen-move Emotional Alchemy grammar are distinct structures.

### Fifteen moves

- **5 Transcend moves:** complete the within-channel progression in the same channel; energy +2.
- **5 Generative translations:** move with the generative cycle; energy +1.
- **5 Control translations:** high-cost precision, protective, stabilizing, or catalytic movement; energy -1.

Control is not bad. Transcend is not automatically superior. A move is judged by fit, timing, cost, and what actually changes.

### Somatic loop

Use the Ontology Alchemy pattern:

> Locate charge in the body → name location and texture → identify emotional channel and face → hold a statement that feels true → breathe and attend → observe shift, non-shift, or new information → choose whether to continue, go deeper, set it down, or act.

The player may stop, witness, or set the work down. Transformation must not be coerced.

### Tension Maps and Transformation Traces

The player lives the tension; the system may preserve only a purpose-bound, correctable representation of what was reported.

- **TensionConfiguration:** formal dissatisfied configuration derived at a named point;
- **Tension Map:** player-facing name for that configuration;
- **TensionSnapshot:** exact fields actually persisted;
- **TransformationTrace:** provenance linking configuration, practice, shift/no-shift, satisfaction, intention, action, artifact, and consequence without requiring every stage.

Bare `TensionVector` is retired. `BARS321TensionVector` survives only as a deprecated compatibility alias for TensionConfiguration. `rootCause` is treated as a player causal hypothesis; `integrationShift` belongs to transformation evidence.

Collection, persistence, derivation, artifact generation, personalization, NPC/world adaptation, collective reuse, sharing, analytics, and model improvement require distinct permissions. Structured extraction is not anonymization.

### Derived movement is a proposal, not a finding

The legacy Epiphany-Bridge binary reuses `translate` and `transcend` with meanings different from the fifteen-move Emotional Alchemy grammar. Treat it as a separate `LegacyEpiphanyMovementMode` namespace; do not join the grammars by label.

A deterministic rule may create a correctable `DerivedMovementProposal` and use an accepted proposal to form a `MovementRoutingPlan` for authored beats. This records what the design intends to invite, not what the player experienced. Actual shift, no-shift, uncertainty, reinterpretation, or refusal belongs to a later `MovementFinding` supported by play evidence and a TransformationRecord.

Correction stops future use of the rejected proposal, flags or regenerates unpublished descendants, versions published descendants, and preserves completed play as history without retaining the rejected interpretation as player truth.

### Yellow Brick Road means recoverability, not infallibility

Retain the Yellow Brick Road as mythic reassurance that **no path has to be wasted**. Do not use it to claim that every result was secretly desired, every divergence was progress, or every harm was necessary.

A destination retains its author and revision history. Routes remain falsifiable. Divergence may become evolution, discovery, error, failure, escape, or noise according to evidence. Some blockers may remain unresolved indefinitely; they may still constrain a particular route without obligating the player to metabolize them. Rest, assistance, accommodation, another route, environmental change, revised destination, abandonment, and composting remain valid.

## 8. Encounter ending and scoped completion

An encounter may end without its move resolving, its quest completing, its arc concluding, or any transformation occurring.

Use scope-specific language:

- participants exit, pause, withdraw, or refuse;
- sessions and encounters end or suspend;
- move invocations resolve, cancel, or are interrupted;
- quests complete when their declared completion conditions are met;
- quests may instead remain incomplete, fail, expire, be declined, abandoned, superseded, or composted;
- arcs conclude, remain open, branch, or are abandoned;
- transformation remains a separate, evidence-bound claim.

Encounter types are composable operative contracts—such as contest, inquiry, relational, transformational, ritual, creative, recovery, governance, or training—not exclusive genre labels. Every completion claim must identify its object, contract, evidence, authority, and contestation state.

> Moves resolve; encounters end; quests complete; arcs conclude.

## 9. BAR lifecycle and composting

A BAR is a captured unit of charged possibility with provenance. It must not become a note that sits forever as unresolved debt.

Possible lifecycle verbs:

- **Capture:** Give charge a minimal form.
- **Plant:** Commit it to active development.
- **Steward:** Carry and refine it intentionally.
- **Graft:** Merge it into a stronger BAR.
- **Store:** Preserve it without current obligation.
- **Fork:** Release another lineage or steward.
- **Harvest:** Extract the learning or useful artifact.
- **Ship:** Declare the present version good enough for use.
- **Compost:** Release the implementation while retaining whatever should nourish future work.

Before preserving an option, ask:

1. What did this teach us?
2. Does more knowledge materially change the next action?
3. Is this alive now, merely possible, or already complete in another form?
4. Should it be planted, shipped, delegated, stored, grafted, forked, harvested, or composted?
5. What is the minimum provenance trace that should remain?

Composting must be recoverable at the level of learning and lineage, even if the active obligation ends.

## 10. Design shadows become playable remediation

| Shadow | Remediating design practice or game |
|---|---|
| Ontological expansion | Decide whether a distinction changes play; promote, merge, reference, or compost it |
| Framework abundance | Choose one framework to lead an encounter while others become observers |
| Meaning density | Require symbols to become meaningful through experience before exposition |
| Architecture before encounter | No new architecture until one player can complete the smallest core loop |
| Designer-player projection | Compare designer pleasure with observed near-neighbor experience |
| Generativity over closure | Reward shipping, composting, delegating, or explicitly pausing |
| Transformation pressure | Treat resting, witnessing, setting down, and no-shift as valid outcomes |
| Systems as distance | Require a somatic checkpoint before additional abstraction |
| Observation without participation | Require one bounded embodied experiment before more analysis |
| Knowledge accumulation | Compost distinctions that do not change a decision |
| Preparation without action | Establish a sufficient-knowledge threshold followed by a concrete move |
| Resource conservation becoming withdrawal | Offer bounded participation with explicit costs, exits, and recovery |
| Competence becoming gatekeeping | Preserve depth while creating low-context entry paths |
| Helper depletion | Include the helper's limits, pleasure, resources, and replenishment in the system model |

## 11. Required translation chain

Every major proposal should be traceable through this chain:

> Designer pleasure and value → design intention → game affordance → player action → emergent dynamic → felt experience → shared meaning → systemic consequence → evidence → ontology revision.

At each transition, state the hypothesis. Do not claim that an intended experience was delivered without evidence.

### Shaman — felt truth, charge, ritual, and symbolic terrain

Ask:

- Where does this ontology feel alive in the body, and where does it become intellectual armor?
- Which emotional channels and states are underrepresented?
- What would make learning the ontology an encounter rather than a lecture?
- What should remain mysterious until earned through play?
- Where must the player be permitted to stop, witness, or remain unchanged?

Deliver a faithful felt-sense translation, at least one somatic comprehension test, and one possible ritualized way another agent could encounter the ontology. Do not treat the ritual as canonical yet.

### Architect — structure, schema, retrieval, and executable use

Ask:

- What classes, relations, constraints, and instances are already present, whether explicit or implicit?
- Which concepts already exist under different names?
- How should an AI retrieve relevant ontology fragments for a specific design problem?
- What provenance, versioning, and confidence fields are necessary?
- Where would machine representation flatten or prematurely collapse a distinction?

Deliver a loss-aware representation map and one worked translation example. Do not choose a minimal schema yet.

### Challenger — falsification, projection, and false depth

Ask:

- What claims are unfalsifiable, self-flattering, or protected by abstraction?
- Where does designer pleasure fail to transfer even to near-neighbor players?
- Where could somatic language, development, or helping become coercive?
- What would reveal that the ontology makes design slower without improving play?
- Which three assumptions should be tested first?

Deliver concrete falsification candidates and comprehension traps, not pruning decisions.

### Regent — canon, boundaries, gates, and completion

Ask:

- Which principles currently present themselves as constitutional, provisional, or unresolved?
- What authority would eventually be required to revise, ratify, fork, deprecate, or compost an ontology node?
- What must be understood and demonstrated before a concept could become canonical?
- What privacy and consent rules govern psychographs and somatic data?
- What is the definition of done for the ingestion and translation pass?

Deliver an authority map and readiness gates for entering later governance. Do not ratify the ontology in this pass.

### Diplomat — users, translation, accessibility, and skilled helping

Ask:

- Who are the first psychographic near-neighbors, and what evidence supports that hypothesis?
- How does the system welcome people with less symbolic, developmental, or systems literacy?
- How can later drafts widen access without condescending or flattening the core?
- How does the design protect helpers from guilt, dependency, depletion, and permanent responsibility?
- What language makes the ontology usable without requiring prior Integral vocabulary?

Deliver one provisional near-neighbor reading and two translation sketches, clearly preserving where meaning changes across users.

### Sage — integration witness and translation steward

Ask:

- Which tensions must remain explicit rather than resolved?
- Can each face accurately restate the source ontology and the other faces' translations?
- Which concepts remain invariant across environments?
- Which concepts change meaning, disappear, or become overemphasized in translation?
- Where do apparent contradictions require more source material rather than synthesis?
- What is the first playable ontology-learning experience that could test comprehension without ratifying a final form?
- What evidence would show readiness for a later operational-synthesis pass?

Sage must create a navigable map of the translations without selecting winners. Integration in this phase means preserving relationships, provenance, and disagreement—not reducing the ontology.

## 13. Required Sage ingestion synthesis

Return:

1. **Source-faithful ontology map** — constitutional principles, hypotheses, concepts, relations, and practices as currently stated.
2. **Six-face translation bundle** — each face's account preserved separately before any cross-face interpretation.
3. **Environment crosswalk** — where each concept lands in existing BARS structures, agent roles, prompts, schemas, and play practices.
4. **Invariant vocabulary** — concepts whose meanings remained stable across all translations.
5. **Translation deltas** — concepts that changed, expanded, contracted, or disappeared in a face's environment.
6. **Apparent equivalences** — possible duplicates or overlaps, explicitly marked as unconfirmed.
7. **Open tensions and ambiguities** — disagreements that must remain visible.
8. **Comprehension tests** — ways to determine whether an agent can apply the ontology rather than merely repeat its terminology.
9. **First playable ingestion loop** — an experience that teaches or tests the ontology without declaring it complete.
10. **Readiness assessment** — evidence present and missing before Phase B operational synthesis should begin.

### Phase B entry conditions

Do not run reduction, canonicalization, or active/reference/compost sorting until:

1. every face can restate the constitutional principles and their relationships without material distortion;
2. the cross-environment map records what was lost or changed in each translation;
3. source conflicts with existing BARS canon are visible and attributed;
4. at least one real design problem has been analyzed using the ingested ontology;
5. Wendell has corrected the council's account of his psychograph and design intentions;
6. the council can distinguish a genuinely redundant concept from two similar concepts doing different work.

Only a separately authorized Phase B council may propose a smallest coherent operational ontology or active/reference/compost placement. Even then, composting is a proposal with provenance—not silent deletion.

## 14. Quality gates

Reject a synthesis that:

- prunes, merges, canonicalizes, or composts concepts during the ingestion pass;
- treats one face's translation as a replacement for the source ontology;
- hides translation loss behind smooth integrative language;
- imposes a minimal schema before mapping the full conceptual terrain;
- treats AQAL as a substitute for evidence;
- ranks whole people by a single developmental level;
- treats Enneagram type as altitude;
- assumes all players share the designer's symbolic literacy;
- confuses engagement with value;
- uses Emotional Alchemy as a good/bad emotion taxonomy;
- requires transformation as the only successful outcome;
- ignores the Game Master's or helper's experience;
- produces an ontology that cannot guide a concrete design decision;
- omits somatic, behavioral, cultural, or systemic verification;
- creates a new framework where an existing canonical object already suffices.
