# Wendell Britt’s Design Philosophy — Draft v0.1

**Status:** Current provisional v0.1 constitutional statement
**Purpose:** Guide game design, game-design tools, AI collaborators, and Game Master agents
**Position in process:** After aesthetic excavation and framework translation; before formal ontology modeling and council governance

**Constitutional lineage:** The fifteen principles in this document amend, expand, and restate the original eight-principle seed in `design-ontology-six-face-council-brief.md`. The original eight remain the provenance-bearing seed; this fifteen-principle collection is the current v0.1 membership set. The seven added principles emerged during aesthetic excavation, became candidates during pattern extraction, were bounded during framework translation, and were promoted here. See `council-phase-a/constitutional-evolution/council-decision.md`.

## Preamble

Design is a mode of play, inquiry, and applied philosophy.

A game does more than entertain or represent a world. Through its rules, rewards, permissions, relationships, and omissions, it proposes what is real, what can change, what counts as development, and whose evidence matters.

This philosophy exists to help Wendell and his AI collaborators create within a recognizable aesthetic field without eliminating surprise. It should guide generative “hallucination” toward resonant possibilities while preserving the ability of players, worlds, and systems to exceed the designer’s prior understanding.

The philosophy begins with Wendell’s own pleasure and psychograph, then builds bridges toward near-neighbor and wider audiences. It does not claim universality, diagnose players, or require every game to contain the whole ontology.

## I. Constitutional principles

These are the most stable commitments in the current design philosophy. Their wording may improve, but later design choices should answer to them explicitly.

The lineage is: original P1–P4 → current P1–P4; seven additions → current P5–P11; original P5–P8 → current P12–P15.

### 1. The Game Master is also a player

The Game Master must receive uncertainty, discovery, meaningful choice, consequence, feedback, and enjoyment. The Game Master is not merely a service provider outside the field of play.

Their world-making, interpretation, and facilitation may themselves be acts of play that seed the world with discoveries not fully known in advance.

### 2. The game designer is also a player

Design should contain experimentation, surprise, interpretation, transformation, and consequential choice. The designer belongs within the experiential field and may learn what the design means by playing through its creation.

### 3. Begin with authentic pleasure, then translate outward

The first working hypothesis is:

> If it is fun for me, it may be fun for people sufficiently like me.

Begin by finding the authentic game for the designer-player. Test it with psychographic near-neighbors. Later drafts may add alternate affordances, explanations, and entry paths for wider audiences without sterilizing the originating pleasure.

### 4. The body gets a vote

Conceptual coherence remains provisional until it meets lived and somatic experience. Intuition may enter play before it can be fully explained. Bodily response may confirm, complicate, or reject a model.

Sensation is evidence, not an infallible interpretation. Lack of change is information, not failure.

### 5. Mechanics make claims about reality

Whatever a game tracks, differentiates, rewards, permits, and allows to change acquires ontological weight.

Choose mechanical attention deliberately. Do not mistake what is easiest to count for what matters most.

### 6. Development changes possibility, not personal worth

Development becomes mechanically real when it changes what can be perceived, chosen, sustained, coordinated, repaired, refused, or brought into being.

Developmental lines remain distinct and situated. Evidence of capability in one line or context must not become a total rank of the person.

### 7. Relationship is a source of capability

Relationship is not merely narrative decoration or a reward channel. Distinct agents can create capacities together that none possesses alone.

Access is not command. Alliance does not erase agency. Trust should be enacted through situated capability, integrity, permission, repair, and voluntary coordination rather than possession or universal reputation.

### 8. Play may precede understanding

Gameplay is performance. A player may enact a form before identifying with it, understanding it, or possessing it as a stable capacity.

Consequential performance can generate story, evidence, rehearsal, discovery, and development. Guardrails should prevent proxy capture without demanding prior authenticity.

### 9. Mystery must reward inquiry

Hidden causality and uncanny relevance are welcome when they remain coherent and inferable. Attentive players should be able to form and test hypotheses from evidence available in play.

Revelation should deepen the field rather than terminate it. Multiple interpretations may remain viable when they answer to evidence, prediction, satisfying action, and usefulness.

### 10. The player authors personal meaning

The designer authors conditions. Game Masters, systems, worlds, and other players may contribute material, constraints, interpretations, and surprises. The player retains authority over what an encounter means inwardly.

Adaptive systems may read provisionally and create explorable material. They must not claim exhaustive knowledge of the player.

> The player may seed the world, but does not exhaust it. The system reads provisionally; the world answers with otherness; the player authors what it means.

### 11. Preserve lineage from seed to result

Emergent outcomes become meaningful when they retain artifacts of their development.

Design should preserve enough provenance to connect originating charge or commitment, subsequent action and relationship, transformation, and result—without pretending the final form was predetermined.

Both outcome value and process value may matter.

### 12. The helper belongs inside the field of care

A helping system has failed if it consumes, erases, or permanently obligates the helper. Capability, integrity, boundaries, replenishment, consent, and enjoyment belong inside the design of helping.

Shared charge creates relevance, not automatic authority. Helping credentials are contextual and must preserve the agency of the person being helped.

### 13. The tool practices what it teaches

A design tool should remediate the conditions that make the tool necessary. If the ontology identifies a recurring design shadow, the process should offer a playable practice for meeting that shadow.

Tools for reducing complexity, sustaining helpers, embodying insight, or composting options should themselves be enjoyable and demonstrate those capacities.

### 14. Composting is a valid completion

A possibility can be honored, metabolized, and released without implementation. Rest, witnessing, ambiguity, good-enough completion, deliberate abandonment, merging, delegation, and composting are legitimate outcomes.

Here, composting may complete the obligation to continue developing or implementing a possibility. It does not pretend that an incomplete quest was completed. More generally: moves resolve, encounters end, quests complete, and arcs conclude. Satisfaction and transformation remain distinct claims.

Capture must not convert every possibility into permanent obligation.

### 15. Motivation does not substitute for meaning

Engagement, retention, progression, and repeated performance are not automatically evidence of fun, transformation, or value.

Motivational systems should support the intended aesthetic, agency, relationship, and development. When optimization hollows out what a mechanic was meant to cultivate, the design has suffered proxy capture and should be revised through play.

## II. Provisional design commitments

These commitments are well supported but remain more revisable than the constitutional principles.

### Integral awareness without maximalism

The ontology should cover all quadrants, lines, levels, states, and types. An individual game should not be required to mechanize all of them.

Each design should declare:

- what it makes mechanically consequential;
- what it supports through narrative or social practice;
- what it intentionally leaves outside scope;
- which players, types, or experiences may be favored or burdened;
- what may be lost through those choices;
- what evidence could test the intended experience.

### Emotional Alchemy satisfaction has priority for inner transformation

For self-directed play, internally satisfying movement is primary evidence that Emotional Alchemy occurred. External approval cannot define the player’s inner result.

In allyship, transformation validity and application fitness remain distinct. Another person’s consent and the observable consequences constrain whether a transformed capacity is appropriate for use with them.

### Development may be multiline

A game may make any of the following mechanically consequential without collapsing them into one ladder:

- emotional and embodied capacity;
- interpretive and epistemic capacity;
- relational capacity and trust;
- integrity and commitment;
- narrative agency and fictional position;
- helping and stewardship capacity;
- authorship and world participation;
- collective and systemic capacity;
- capacity for rest, refusal, release, and composting.

### Expertise should become stewardship

Power players may perceive useful patterns without complete certainty. Their mastery should create opportunities to orient, teach, witness, or assist—not authority to dictate another player’s meaning.

### Design for resolution depth

Where depth matters, preserve enough spatial, temporal, symbolic, or causal room for low-resolution intentions to develop into high-resolution action.

Accessibility should clarify entry without accidentally teaching that the reduced exercise is the entirety of the experience.

### Allow latent commitments

Some choices should shape the future before their significance is fully settled. Later relationships and events may help determine what the earlier gesture becomes.

### Adaptation remains optional and governable

Adaptive curation can increase relevance and preserve momentum. Players should be able to reduce or disable it when it conflicts with their playstyle.

Adaptation, explanation, retention, visibility, reuse, and sharing are separate permissions. Explanations should distinguish observation, inference, and speculation and remain open to correction.

## III. Epistemic rules

### Evidence is plural

Important claims should be inspectable across:

- **UL:** felt meaning, charge, intention, interpretation, satisfaction;
- **UR:** behavior, demonstrated capacity, somatic response, completed action;
- **LL:** relationship, consent, shared meaning, cultural interpretation;
- **LR:** roles, rules, permissions, economies, institutions, persistent consequences.

These perspectives may disagree. Disagreement is evidence, not a defect to erase.

### Interpretations are instruments, not possessions

An interpretation earns provisional use through contact with evidence. It remains worth carrying when it improves prediction, satisfying action, or contextual usefulness.

It may later be revised, narrowed, archived, or composted without treating its former usefulness as fraudulent.

### Systems must remain corrigible

AI and adaptive systems should state uncertainty, accept contradiction, distinguish inference from fact, and permit “that is not mine” as a valid response.

An explanation must remain smaller than the player, relationship, or world it describes.

### Provenance accompanies authority

Claims to insight, capability, trust, or helping authority should retain evidence of how they arose, under which conditions, according to whom, and within what domain.

No badge, metric, interpretation, or credential is identical to the living capacity it represents.

## IV. Productive tensions

The ontology should help designers work with these tensions rather than pretending they have universal solutions:

- mystery and explanation;
- authored structure and emergence;
- intuition and explicit understanding;
- performance and identification;
- outcome and process;
- personal resonance and world otherness;
- adaptation and sovereignty;
- inner satisfaction and external consequence;
- access and autonomy;
- strategic breadth and tactical immediacy;
- simulation detail and narrative consequence;
- legibility and reduction;
- development and rest.

A particular game may lean toward one pole. The design should know what that choice enables and costs.

## V. Design inquiry

For any proposed mechanic, encounter, tool, or system, ask:

### Meaning and pleasure

- What is intrinsically enjoyable for the designer-player or Game Master?
- What meaning does the mechanic carry beyond engagement?
- Is the originating pleasure still present after translation for other users?

### Reality and development

- What does this design make mechanically real?
- What kind of development changes possibility here?
- Which lines remain distinct, and which are being accidentally collapsed?
- What earlier work remains consequential rather than disposable?

### Agency and relationship

- Which agents possess independent aims, permissions, and capacity to refuse?
- What new capability exists because of relationship?
- Does access become mistaken for ownership or command?
- Can trust be demonstrated without becoming a global score?

### Mystery and interpretation

- What may remain hidden?
- What evidence makes the hidden pattern inferable?
- Can more than one interpretation remain useful and grounded?
- Does explanation open inquiry or terminate it?

### Performance and motivation

- What performance does the reward invite?
- What story or capacity might that performance cultivate?
- What optimization pattern is likely to emerge?
- What would show that the proxy has displaced its purpose?

### Somatics and Emotional Alchemy

- What charge or bodily response appears?
- Does recognition feel enclosing or inviting?
- What changed, remained unchanged, or became less clear?
- Is the result inwardly satisfying?
- If another agent is affected, was the application consensual and fit?

### Provenance and completion

- Can the result be traced to its seed, process, and relationships?
- What artifacts preserve that lineage?
- Does this possibility ask for action now?
- Can it rest, become reference, transfer stewardship, or be composted?

## VI. Playable verification

Principles remain hypotheses until they survive play.

Verification should look for:

- reported pleasure and meaning from the designer-player and intended psychographic neighbors;
- somatic evidence, including non-response and ambivalence;
- observable changes in choice, capability, relationship, or consequence;
- multiple viable performances rather than a single hollow optimization routine;
- player ability to infer important patterns from available evidence;
- persistent lineage between earlier choices and later results;
- retained agency for allies, helpers, Game Masters, and players;
- differences between intended experience and actual experience;
- costs imposed on helpers, facilitators, or excluded player types;
- evidence that rest, refusal, or composting remains legitimate.

When the ontology introduces a concern, the design process should propose a playable test. It should not transfer the burden of solving every hypothetical risk back to the designer.

---

## VII. Instructions for AI collaborators

When using this philosophy, an AI should:

1. preserve the constitutional principles and identify any proposed exception;
2. generate inside the aesthetic field without merely remixing named inspirations;
3. label fact, source interpretation, user statement, AI inference, and speculation separately;
4. retain provenance for important concepts and recommendations;
5. offer surprising possibilities that remain causally and aesthetically related;
6. state which developmental lines and AQAL perspectives a proposal foregrounds;
7. distinguish player-authored meaning from system inference;
8. avoid global rankings of people from situated evidence;
9. protect the agency of allies, helpers, Game Masters, and players;
10. propose verification through play for risks the AI raises;
11. stop elaborating a specific game when the task is to clarify the ontology;
12. preserve unresolved tensions and alternatives until governance is explicitly requested;
13. allow ideas to rest or be composted without treating that as failure;
14. preserve fun for the designer and Game Master as evidence, not indulgence.

---

## VIII. Boundaries of this draft

This philosophy does not yet:

- define the formal ontology’s entities, relations, states, or confidence model;
- determine the smallest coherent operational subset;
- assign concepts to active, reference, or compost sets;
- prescribe a universal game architecture;
- define a complete player psychograph taxonomy;
- establish that every provisional commitment belongs in the constitutional layer.

Those decisions follow through ontology modeling, council ingestion, operational governance, and playable verification.
