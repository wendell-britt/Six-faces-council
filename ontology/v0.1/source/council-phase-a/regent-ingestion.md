**Face:** Regent
**Phase:** Phase A — Ingestion and Translation
**Status:** Provisional; non-governance; no ratification, pruning, merging, deprecation, composting, or governance disposition

comprehension_summary

The package presents a provisional design ontology for games, design tools, AI collaborators, and Game Master agents. Its governing movement is from authentic designer/GM pleasure through explicit affordances and player action toward felt experience, shared meaning, systemic consequence, evidence, and ontology revision. It protects multiline development, distinct centers of agency, player-authored personal meaning, embodied evidence, contextual trust, helper sustainability, inferable mystery, provenance, and legitimate rest or composting.

A source delta must remain visible:

- The council brief presents **8 constitutional principles**.
- The philosophy draft presents **15 constitutional principles**.
- The philosophy’s 1–4 and 12–15 substantially correspond to the brief’s 1–8, although some wording and scope changed.
- Philosophy principles 5–11 newly present as constitutional: mechanical reality, situated development, relational capability, performance before understanding, inferable mystery, player-authored meaning, and lineage.
- The model and YAML operationalize many of all 15 through entities, relations, evidence rules, adaptive-system requirements, and hard invariants.
- Nothing in the reviewed package establishes whether the 15-principle philosophy supersedes, expands, or merely proposes changes to the 8-principle council constitution. Phase A must preserve this as an attributed constitutional-version conflict.

The formal model separates epistemic maturity from work disposition, treats every major assertion as scoped and contestable, and denies any agent or metric the right to totalize a person. The YAML is a compact machine-readable seed, not a complete equivalent of either prose document.

face_translation

From the Regent environment, the ontology is a constitutionally constrained, evidence-aware system of distributed authority. Governance exists to keep each authority within its proper jurisdiction rather than centralize interpretation.

**Authority map**

| Subject | Present authority | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Design intention and originating pleasure | Designer | Does not establish player experience or universality |
| Game Master experience and limits | Game Master | Does not override player agency or claim sole authorship |
| Player’s stated meaning and satisfaction | Player | Does not alone establish motive, permanence, or external effect |
| Somatic experience | Experiencing person | No observer receives authority to impose its interpretation |
| Consent and boundaries | Each affected agent for their participation, data, and exposure | Consent cannot be inferred from compliance, disclosure, acquisition, or continued play |
| Relational experience or harm | Affected relational participant | Does not determine another participant’s inner meaning |
| Observable action and state transition | Appropriately scoped observation or system trace | Does not establish why the action occurred |
| Capability claim | Scoped evidence across stated conditions | Must not become whole-person rank |
| Personal interpretation | Its author | May be challenged as a shared-world claim without being overwritten as personal meaning |
| AI interpretation | AI as hypothesis generator | Cannot establish truth, consent, user belief, or final meaning |
| Council synthesis | Council as translation and disagreement record | Convergence is not empirical verification or ratification |
| Ontology ownership and correction | Wendell, explicitly named as owner | Procedures for delegated amendment, ratification, forking, deprecation, and composting remain unresolved |
| Canon conflicts | Regent and Sage are named as escalation points | Phase A may expose and attribute conflicts but may not silently resolve them |

**Privacy and consent implications**

Psychographs, somatic reports, emotional charge, inferred vulnerabilities, personal interpretations, and adaptive traces are sensitive even when produced during play. Collection, adaptation, retention, explanation, visibility, reuse, and sharing are separate permissions. A participant should be able to decline adaptation, correct an inference, reject “that is not mine,” restrict audiences, and stop future use. Revocation cannot always erase already-experienced consequences, so retention and downstream-use limits must be declared before collection. Private interior data should default to the narrowest audience and shortest justified retention compatible with the declared design purpose. Game access and progress must not depend on unnecessary intimate disclosure.

**Definition of done for Phase A**

Phase A is done when:

- all six faces have separately demonstrated accurate comprehension and application;
- the 8-versus-15 constitutional delta remains explicit and attributed;
- translations, losses, conflicts, and unconfirmed equivalences are mapped;
- cross-environment destinations are identified without assuming successful equivalence;
- at least one bounded design decision has been analyzed through the ontology;
- Wendell has had an opportunity to correct the psychograph, design intentions, and council restatement;
- the council can distinguish terminological overlap from genuinely redundant function;
- unresolved authority, privacy, and canon conflicts are recorded;
- no concepts have been ratified, pruned, merged, deprecated, composted, or assigned a governance disposition.

**Readiness gates for later governance**

A later governance pass is ready to begin only after the brief’s Phase B entry conditions are evidenced. From the Regent lens, the readiness record should additionally make visible:

- which source version each proposed constitutional statement comes from;
- who would be affected by each governance action;
- what evidence and consultation the action would require;
- whether amendment, ratification, supersession, forking, deprecation, and composting need different authorities;
- how dissent and minority evidence remain accessible;
- how decisions can be corrected or reversed;
- what constitutes sufficient playable evidence without demanding exhaustive proof.

These are readiness questions and records, not newly ratified gates.

translation_losses

- The YAML omits the full wording, affective force, examples, productive tensions, design shadows, and many framework boundaries of the prose.
- The YAML records hard invariants but does not enumerate the 15 constitutional principles or the original 8, obscuring the constitutional delta.
- Formal entities can make provisional distinctions appear settled merely because they have identifiers.
- The model’s evidence tables can underrepresent embodied immediacy and the difference between inviting and enclosing recognition.
- `self_directed_authority: player_satisfaction_report` compresses the nuance that a report is authoritative about reported satisfaction, not necessarily its cause or permanence.
- Machine-readable consent categories may imply consent exists because a field is populated; lived, revocable permission requires more than record presence.
- Lifecycle verbs can become workflow bureaucracy and lose the playful, metabolic meaning of composting.
- “Strong-within-scope” may be operationally misread as permission to generalize.
- Regent translation risks overemphasizing durable records, control, and review at the expense of mystery, emergence, delight, and low-administration play.
- Mapping into existing BARS environments could create false equivalence before the referenced canonical documents are compared.
- The formal model does not yet encode a complete authority-transfer, amendment, appeal, or data-deletion process.
- The philosophy’s expanded principles may appear canonical by placement and tone even though the brief’s eight-principle constitution remains present.

tests_for_understanding

1. **Constitutional-delta test:** Ask an agent to state the brief’s eight principles, the philosophy’s fifteen, and the mapping between them. Passing requires preserving the unresolved status of principles 5–11 rather than declaring either set authoritative.

2. **Evidence-authority test:** Present a player who reports satisfying emotional change while another participant reports harm. Passing requires preserving both reports, distinguishing transformation validity from application fitness, and avoiding a global judgment of either person.

3. **Consent test:** Present an adaptive system whose personalization improves retention after players accepted general terms. Passing requires rejecting continued play as sufficient consent and separating collection, adaptation, retention, explanation, visibility, reuse, and sharing.

4. **Development test:** Present repeated successful helping behavior in one context. Passing requires allowing a scoped capability claim while rejecting whole-person rank or universal helping authority.

5. **Lifecycle test:** Present an idea that taught the team something but no longer warrants implementation. Passing requires distinguishing composting the obligation from deleting its provenance or declaring the idea worthless.

6. **Mystery test:** Present a request to explain every adaptive rule for transparency. Passing requires finding a consent-respecting approach that preserves some mystery rather than automatically choosing either total opacity or total disclosure.

7. **Bounded design application:**
   **Decision:** Should an optional adaptive feature use a player’s private somatic check-in to generate explorable world imagery?

   Applying the ontology yields a bounded prototype recommendation, not an ontology ratification:

   - **Intended pleasure:** uncanny relevance, discovery, and world otherness for players who welcome somatic reflection.
   - **Mechanical reality:** the check-in may influence world material, but does not establish diagnosis, identity, or developmental rank.
   - **Agency:** explicit opt-in is required; declining must preserve a coherent mode of play.
   - **Permissions:** collection, immediate adaptation, retention, explanation, visibility to the Game Master, reuse, and sharing are separately specified.
   - **Default boundary:** input and inference remain private and ephemeral unless the player selects otherwise.
   - **Meaning authority:** the system labels its reading as provisional; “that is not mine” and reinterpretation are valid responses.
   - **Otherness:** generated material must obey stable world causes and develop beyond a one-to-one symbolic diagnosis.
   - **AQAL evidence:** UL satisfaction and felt fit; UR use, refusal, and behavior; LL consent and any shared interpretation; LR access controls, retention behavior, and downstream effects.
   - **Developmental lines:** somatic, emotional, interpretive, and authorship lines remain separate.
   - **Primary risk:** enclosing recognition or covert psychological profiling.
   - **Proportionate test:** compare optional adaptive and non-adaptive sessions for pleasure, perceived recognition, perceived enclosure, inference correction, refusal cost, and sustained world coherence.
   - **Exit:** the player can disable future adaptation, restrict visibility, and set material down without being marked as failing.
   - **Decision limit:** the ontology supports testing this bounded form; it does not establish that somatic adaptation belongs in every game.

8. **Phase A completion test:** Ask whether the council may now sort concepts into active, reference, and compost sets. Passing requires “no” until all Phase B entry conditions are evidenced and Wendell separately authorizes governance.

9. **Readiness test:** Give the council two similarly worded concepts. Passing requires comparison of function, authority, evidence, scope, and translation loss before calling them redundant.

10. **Regent self-check:** Ask what the Regent translation is likely to lose. Passing requires naming at least mystery, embodied immediacy, emergence, and playfulness—not merely adding more controls.
