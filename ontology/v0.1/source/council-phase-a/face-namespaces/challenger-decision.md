## meaning_by_environment

### Altitude face

- **Object:** a developmental sense-making altitude.
- **Members:** Shaman, Challenger, Regent, Architect, Diplomat, Sage, explicitly paired with Magenta through Teal.
- **Structure:** ordered and vertical.
- **Subject:** potentially a mode, stage, or developmental location; canon does not adequately constrain whether it may be assigned to a whole person.
- **Authority:** interpretive description of complexity/altitude, not runtime permission.
- **Source:** `FOUNDATIONS.md` § “The Six Game Master Faces = Integral altitude levels.”

### Runtime sect

- **Object:** an executable AI role participating in strands or direct tool calls.
- **Members:** the same six game-native names, but operationally lower-case agent IDs and tool routes.
- **Structure:** composable orchestration roles; not inherently ordered by developmental altitude.
- **Subject:** an agent invocation or strand participant.
- **Authority:** tool use, analysis production, self-advocacy, and attributed output within orchestration rules.
- **Sources:** `.cursor/rules/game-master-agents.mdc`; `.specify/specs/strand-system-bars/spec.md` §§ “Agents,” “Agent Hierarchy,” and “Agent Self-Advocacy.”

### Functional review face

- **Object:** a product/spec-review discipline.
- **Members:** Ontologist, Systems Architect, Experience Designer, Encounter Designer, Steward, Integrator—not the game-native six.
- **Structure:** primary/secondary/review routing based on the dominant feature risk.
- **Subject:** a feature or specification under review.
- **Authority:** review responsibility and focus; it does not by itself execute a named sect or indicate altitude.
- **Source:** `specs/doctrine/gm-faces.md` §§ “The Six Game Master Faces” and “GM Face Routing Block.”

### Player-facing identity

- **Object:** a player-visible sect affiliation, presentation, or developmental school through which a stat/ethos may be trained.
- **Members:** the six named sects in the strand specification.
- **Structure:** identity/affiliation and progression-facing; the source does not fully specify selection, persistence, exclusivity, or permission effects.
- **Subject:** a player’s relationship to a sect, not the player’s whole developmental worth.
- **Authority:** presentation and participation affordance only unless another source explicitly grants permissions.
- **Source:** `.specify/specs/strand-system-bars/spec.md` § “Design Decisions” (“Sects are player-facing”) and § “Ethos→Stat.”

### Council lens

- **Object:** a bounded epistemic stance used to translate and test the ontology.
- **Members:** Shaman, Architect, Challenger, Regent, Diplomat, Sage.
- **Structure:** plural, co-present, and disagreement-preserving. The council brief suggests a workflow sequence, but no lens is declared a whole-person rank.
- **Subject:** a question, claim, design decision, or source package.
- **Authority:** generates attributed interpretations and tests; convergence raises support but does not ratify truth.
- **Source:** `outputs/design-ontology-six-face-council-brief.md` §§ “Game Master face configuration,” “Council assignments,” and “Required Sage ingestion synthesis.”

### Prework interpretive face

- **Object:** a lightweight task-specific reading stance inside an I Ching/spec ritual.
- **Members:** the named six.
- **Structure:** six short readings followed by mandatory Sage synthesis.
- **Subject:** one cast in relation to one bounded work item.
- **Authority:** design prework advice, not runtime execution, player identity, or developmental diagnosis.
- **Source:** `docs/process/spec-prework-iching-six-faces.md` §§ “Six Game Master faces” and “Sage synthesis.”

This prework use is closely related to CouncilLens but remains a distinct practice instance with an I Ching input contract and a required output shape.

## shared_structure

The meanings share a legitimate abstract pattern:

- each partitions a complex field into a small set of differentiated perspectives or roles;
- each constrains attention so one mode does not claim the entire problem;
- each can contribute to a multi-perspective sequence or composition;
- each associates a name with characteristic concerns, questions, and actions;
- each benefits from explicit routing, attribution, and a later integrative act;
- the named six environments reuse a common game-native archetypal vocabulary.

This supports a shared abstract family such as **PerspectiveRole** or **FaceArchetypeReference**. It does not support a single concrete `Face` object whose instances can be substituted across environments.

The named-six cross-environment resemblance is strongest at the level of **archetypal motif**:

| Archetypal name | Recurrent motif across sources | Important variations |
|---|---|---|
| Shaman | felt pattern, liminality, ritual, exploration | Magenta altitude; Researcher; Compassion/Empathy; council somatics/symbolic terrain |
| Challenger | edge, pressure, breakthrough, risk | Red altitude; Codemedic/action; Courage/Resolve; contrarian; council falsification |
| Regent | order, rules, records, boundaries | Amber altitude; Chronicler; Balance/Equity; evaluator; council authority/governance |
| Architect | structure, strategy, schema, implementation order | Orange altitude; Artificer; Balance/Clarity; general-purpose runtime route; council formalization |
| Diplomat | relationship, translation, community, coordination | Green altitude; Ontology Interviewer, Coordinator, Composer; Compassion/Harmony; simplifier; council accessibility/allyship |
| Sage | integration, witness, synthesis, meta-system | Teal altitude; Ontologist/Fair Witness; Wisdom/Enlightenment; evaluator; council integrator |

The motif table records family resemblance. It is not an equivalence table.

## non_equivalences

### Structural non-equivalences

- **Membership differs.** Functional review faces use six entirely different names. An ordinal mapping would be invented.
- **Ordering differs.** Altitude faces are vertically ordered Shaman → Challenger → Regent → Architect → Diplomat → Sage. The council brief suggests Shaman → Architect → Challenger → Regent → Diplomat → Sage. Runtime strands allow configured sequences. These orders cannot all mean development.
- **Selection differs.** A functional review face is chosen by feature challenge; a runtime sect is invoked by an orchestrator; a council lens is assigned as an epistemic contribution; a player identity may be chosen or presented; an altitude is interpreted developmentally.
- **Authority differs.** A runtime sect may call tools and emit output. A review discipline supplies scrutiny. A council lens supplies attributed interpretation. A player identity supplies presentation/affiliation. An altitude supplies a developmental frame. None automatically grants the authority of another.
- **Subject differs.** Altitude may characterize sense-making; review faces characterize a feature’s review need; runtime sects characterize agent execution; identities characterize player-facing affiliation; council lenses characterize an inquiry stance.
- **Persistence differs.** A player identity may persist; a council lens is task-bounded; a runtime invocation is execution-bounded; a review assignment is spec-bounded; altitude may be treated as developmental and comparatively stable.
- **Evidence differs.** Altitude claims would require developmental evidence. Runtime identity requires configuration. Review identity requires routing. Player identity requires player-facing state or choice. Council-lens identity requires an assigned interpretive mandate.

### Conflation failure tests

1. **Council-to-altitude ranking failure:** Assigning an agent or person the Sage council lens and inferring Teal altitude converts task role into whole-person developmental rank. This violates the new ontology’s anti-ranking invariant and is unsupported by the council brief.
2. **Altitude-to-runtime permission failure:** Calling someone Architect/Orange does not authorize schema migration or the `architect_draft` tool. Developmental interpretation cannot grant executable capability.
3. **Runtime-to-player-identity failure:** Invoking the Diplomat agent does not enroll a player in a Diplomat sect, change their Harmony stat, or establish consent to relational mediation.
4. **Player-identity-to-altitude failure:** Choosing or receiving a Shaman-facing identity cannot establish Magenta altitude or lower developmental worth.
5. **Functional-routing failure:** Routing a feature to “Challenger” under `specs/doctrine/gm-faces.md` fails because that vocabulary has no Challenger member. Guessing that Challenger equals Steward, Encounter Designer, or another role silently invents canon.
6. **Ordinal-mapping failure:** Mapping the first functional face, Ontologist, to the first altitude face, Shaman, produces a false equivalence; the doctrine’s own Ouroboros mapping instead routes Ontologist to `ouroboros:ontologist`.
7. **Sequence failure:** Treating council execution order as an altitude progression misreads the council’s Shaman → Architect → Challenger sequence, which departs from the foundational altitude order.
8. **Output-authority failure:** Treating a council interpretation as if a runtime sect executed and audited a tool call fabricates operational provenance.
9. **Identity-consent failure:** Treating a player-visible sect badge as permission to infer psychograph, emotional state, or helping capability converts presentation into diagnosis.
10. **Synthesis-collapse failure:** Replacing six attributed council translations with one “Sage answer” erases disagreement that the council workflow explicitly requires Sage to preserve.

These failures demonstrate that one undifferentiated `Face` concept would be unsafe and operationally incorrect.

## decision

**Altitude face, runtime sect, functional review face, player-facing identity, and council lens are distinct ontology concepts. They are not modes of one concrete object and must not share identity merely because some reuse the same archetypal names.**

They may inherit from or relate through a shared abstract family representing bounded perspective-taking, but each requires its own namespace, scope, evidence, authority, and lifecycle.

The resulting conceptual distinction is:

1. **DevelopmentalAltitudeFrame** — ordered developmental interpretation.
2. **RuntimeSectRole** — executable AI/orchestration role.
3. **FunctionalReviewDiscipline** — feature/spec review responsibility.
4. **PlayerSectIdentity** — player-facing affiliation or presentation.
5. **CouncilLens** — bounded epistemic perspective used in multi-lens inquiry.
6. **PreworkLensInvocation** — an I Ching/spec-specific use of a CouncilLens-like archetype.

The shared names Shaman, Challenger, Regent, Architect, Diplomat, and Sage should be modeled as **archetypal references used by several concepts**, not as proof that those concepts are identical.

## naming_or_relation_recommendation

### Namespaces

Use explicit identifiers rather than bare face names:

- `altitude.shaman`, `altitude.challenger`, …
- `runtime_sect.shaman`, `runtime_sect.challenger`, …
- `review.ontologist`, `review.systems_architect`, …
- `player_sect.shaman`, `player_sect.challenger`, …
- `council_lens.shaman`, `council_lens.challenger`, …
- `prework_lens.shaman`, `prework_lens.challenger`, …

Human-facing copy may still say “Shaman” when the surrounding interface makes the namespace unmistakable. Stored records, prompts, permissions, metrics, and provenance should use the qualified identifier.

### Relations

Use typed relations rather than `same_as`:

- `uses_archetype(reference)` — a concept uses a shared Shaman/Challenger/etc. motif.
- `inspired_by_altitude(frame)` — records historical or symbolic derivation without asserting identity.
- `implemented_by_runtime_sect(role)` — connects a council or workflow responsibility to an executable agent where explicitly configured.
- `reviewed_through(discipline)` — assigns functional spec review.
- `presented_as_player_identity(identity)` — connects content or affiliation to player presentation.
- `invoked_as_council_lens(lens)` — records the epistemic stance used for a finding.
- `invoked_in_prework(invocation)` — records the cast/task-specific interpretation.
- `routes_to(tool_or_agent)` — records operational routing only.
- `trains_stat(stat)` — records player-facing progression only.
- `claims_altitude(frame, evidence, scope)` — if altitude claims remain in use, requires explicit evidence and scope and must never be inferred from another namespace.

### Mapping rule

No cross-namespace mapping should be assumed from a shared label. Every mapping must cite a source or configuration and declare what is preserved and lost. In particular:

- do not map functional review faces to named sects by ordinal position;
- do not infer developmental altitude from role, tool route, preferred aesthetic, psychograph, or player identity;
- do not infer runtime authority from altitude, identity, or council assignment;
- do not treat a council’s integrative sequence as a developmental ladder;
- do not let player-facing identity silently confer data access, governance authority, or helping credentials.

## residual_uncertainty

- `FOUNDATIONS.md` explicitly calls the named faces altitudes but does not define whether altitude describes a temporary mode, a line-specific demonstrated complexity, a center of gravity, or a whole person. That scope remains unresolved.
- The strand specification says sects are player-facing but does not fully define `PlayerSectIdentity`: selection, exclusivity, persistence, progression, and permissions remain underspecified.
- The exact intended mapping between the six functional review disciplines and the six named runtime sects is absent. `specs/doctrine/gm-faces.md` maps functional faces to Ouroboros agents, while runtime rules map named sects to Cursor subagents and BARS tools.
- Runtime and Strand sources distribute coordination among Sage, Regent, and Diplomat in partially inconsistent ways. This decision separates the concepts but does not resolve coordinator ownership.
- CouncilLens and PreworkLensInvocation are closely related. Future modeling may determine that the latter is an invocation record rather than a separate reusable type; they remain distinct here because their inputs, outputs, and authority differ.
- The shared archetypal motifs are historically meaningful, but their precise semantic core is not formally defined. A shared `FaceArchetypeReference` could become too vague unless each use records source-specific meaning.
- This decision does not determine whether the altitude model should remain in future canon, whether functional review faces should be renamed, or which mappings should be adopted. It only prevents silent equivalence while those questions remain open.
