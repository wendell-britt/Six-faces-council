# Composting — Reconstruction and Ontology Decision

**Date:** 2026-10-12

**Status:** Binding v0.1 ontology decision

**Scope:** Release, residue, lineage, recoverability, reuse, regeneration, and the end of active obligation

**Does not authorize:** Bulk active/reference/compost sorting, deletion of another agent's material, or reuse of personal material without permission

## Question resolved

Can composting end with release alone, or must retained material eventually generate new fuel, learning, or form?

## Reconstruction: what exists

The sources use **composting** in two compatible but non-identical ways.

1. **Creative regeneration:** BARS `FOUNDATIONS.md` describes creative composting as metabolizing past creative energy into new form and generating Vibeulons rather than ghosts.
2. **Release from obligation:** The design constitution permits a possibility to be honored and released without implementation. The model and council records use composting to end active obligation while preserving appropriate learning and lineage.

The first describes one possible result of compost. The second defines the lifecycle function the ontology needs. Requiring regeneration would turn release into a delayed production obligation and recreate the backlog pressure composting exists to remediate.

## Reconstruction: where composting operates

- **Personal creative work:** a creator releases an earlier idea, artifact, identity investment, or unfinished possibility.
- **BAR lifecycle:** a kernel or expression leaves active stewardship without being declared worthless or completed.
- **Quest lifecycle:** a quest receives a noncompletion disposition; its original contract remains unmet unless composting was itself a completion condition.
- **Design governance:** a proposal, concept, branch, or implementation obligation leaves the active set.
- **Emotional or somatic practice:** a player may set material down without promising transformation or future use.
- **Data and provenance:** retained residue, source material, and later reuse remain subject to privacy, consent, retention, and deletion rules.

## Reconstruction: when composting occurs

1. A `CompostCandidate` identifies material or obligation proposed for release.
2. A `CompostingDecision` records scope, authority, affected parties, and intended disposition.
3. A `CompostingDisposition` ends the named active obligation at the effective time.
4. A `CompostRecord` preserves only the lineage and decision metadata that may legitimately remain.
5. Optional `CompostResidue` identifies material permitted to remain available for reference or possible reuse.
6. A later `RegenerationEvent` records actual reuse or new growth. It is a new event with its own authority and provenance, not proof that the earlier composting was incomplete until then.

## Decision

### 1. Composting completes release

Composting is:

> A governed lifecycle disposition that ends a named active obligation while preserving only the lineage, learning, or reusable residue that is appropriate and permitted.

The disposition succeeds when the obligation ends. It does not require:

- a new artifact;
- a lesson;
- emotional transformation;
- Vibeulon generation;
- later reuse;
- recovery of value;
- proof that release was strategically optimal.

Creative regeneration remains a possible downstream event, not a completion criterion.

### 2. Composting is not deletion, archival, or original completion

- **Deletion** removes material according to a deletion authority and policy.
- **Archival/reference** preserves material for expected consultation without active implementation.
- **Abandonment** ends pursuit without necessarily preserving a compost decision or residue.
- **Supersession** installs a successor while preserving the predecessor relation.
- **Completion** satisfies the object's declared completion contract.
- **Composting** ends the obligation to continue and defines what, if anything, may remain.

A composted quest is not thereby completed. A composted claim is not thereby false. A composted BAR is not thereby worthless. A deleted record is not secretly retained as compost.

### 3. Residue is optional and permission-bound

A `CompostRecord` should preserve the minimum legitimate trace of the decision: target identity, released obligation, authority, effective time, reason category if supplied, recoverability level, and permitted downstream relations.

A `CompostResidue` may include:

- a lesson;
- reusable pattern;
- excerpt or source artifact;
- unresolved question;
- warning or failed test;
- relationship or dependency map;
- aesthetic fragment;
- provenance-only tombstone.

No residue is required when privacy, safety, deletion rights, burden, or irrelevance makes retention inappropriate. “Preserve lineage” means preserve the minimum lawful and useful lineage, not retain everything forever.

### 4. Regeneration is a new event

A `RegenerationEvent` occurs only when permitted residue later contributes to a new BAR, artifact, practice, decision, or other form. It records:

- source CompostRecord and residue;
- new author or steward;
- purpose and permissions;
- transformation or selection performed;
- new object's identity and lineage;
- whether the original material remains available.

Regeneration does not retroactively justify retention, prove that composting was wise, or turn the new object into the same object.

### 5. Release must be behaviorally real

After composting, the system must stop treating the released obligation as active. Unless an explicit reconsideration policy says otherwise, it must stop:

- backlog pressure and overdue status;
- guilt, shame, or failure messaging;
- progress penalties;
- required review;
- automatic resurfacing;
- agent prompts to complete or metabolize it;
- counting it as unfinished active work.

If the system continues these behaviors, it has archived or deferred the work rather than composted it.

### 6. Reconsideration creates a new obligation

Permitted residue may later be reviewed, but reactivation requires an explicit `ReactivationDecision`. It records who reactivated it, why, under what authority, and which new obligation begins. The earlier composting remains valid history.

### 7. Authority is scoped

- An agent may release its own implementation obligation where no shared contract requires continuation.
- The steward of a BAR or shared project may propose composting within their domain, subject to affected commitments and governance.
- A data subject controls retention and reuse of their personal material within applicable constraints.
- One participant cannot compost another participant's rights, consent boundary, active commitment, or account of meaning.
- Shared obligations require the authority named by their contract; silence or inactivity is not automatic composting.

### 8. Avoidance is a separate interpretation

Composting may be wise, premature, protective, avoidant, necessary, or ambiguous. The disposition and the interpretation remain separate. An AI may propose a risk or invite a proportionate check, but it cannot invalidate release by diagnosing avoidance from discomfort, incompletion, or lack of regeneration.

### 9. Recoverability has levels

`CompostRecoverability` is explicit:

- `none`: material is deleted or intentionally unrecoverable;
- `decision_only`: only a tombstone and disposition remain;
- `lineage_only`: origin and typed relationships remain without source content;
- `residue_available`: permitted fragments or learning remain;
- `source_restorable`: original source may be restored;
- `implementation_restorable`: working implementation can be resumed.

Recoverability level does not change whether active obligation ended.

## AI guardrails

An AI collaborator must not:

1. require new output, insight, transformation, or fuel before accepting composting;
2. keep composted work in active backlogs, reminders, progress penalties, or completion prompts;
3. call a composted quest completed unless its contract made composting sufficient;
4. treat composting as proof that the material lacked value or truth;
5. retain private source content under the pretext of preserving lineage;
6. reuse CompostResidue without purpose-specific permission;
7. treat later regeneration as the same object or as retroactive justification for retention;
8. diagnose release as avoidance without independent evidence;
9. reactivate composted work silently;
10. use this decision as authorization for bulk active/reference/compost governance.

## Relationship to prior decisions

- The completion boundary distinguishes ending active obligation from completing the original quest.
- Yellow Brick Road recoverability permits abandonment and composting without declaring every path successful.
- The Roadblock decision removes any duty to metabolize a blocker before release.
- BAR scope distinguishes the kernel from its expressions and permits BARRoleRelease without lineage erasure.
- Tension and session-depth decisions require separate permission for retention, derivation, and reuse of personal material.

## Result

Compost may nourish future growth, but it does not owe the garden a harvest. Release is the completed lifecycle act. Residue and regeneration remain optional, governed, and separately evidenced.
