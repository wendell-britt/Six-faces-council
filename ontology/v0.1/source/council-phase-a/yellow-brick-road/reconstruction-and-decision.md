# Yellow Brick Road — Reconstruction and Ontology Decision

**Decision date:** 2026-10-12
**Status:** Provisional ontology ruling from source reconstruction
**Scope:** “You can never go the wrong way,” divergence, blockers, and unresolved material
**Implementation status:** Descriptive only; no BARS code was changed

## Source reconstruction

`FOUNDATIONS.md` makes four related claims:

1. a road connects a present Point A to a desired Point B;
2. BARS carries people and groups along that road;
3. blockers can be converted into bricks and fuel through Emotional Alchemy;
4. forks are evolution rather than failure, so “you can never go the wrong way.”

It also says movement is limited by identifying and metabolizing blockers and that there is no shortcut past them.

The design ontology independently establishes that:

- player-authored inner meaning cannot be overwritten by system interpretation;
- failed predictions, harm, no-shift, refusal, rest, retreat, and abandonment remain real findings;
- quests complete only under their declared completion conditions;
- possibility may be honored and released without implementation;
- changing a destination is different from reaching the original destination.

## Governing distinction

> **No path is wasted does not mean no move is mistaken.**

The Yellow Brick Road is a **mythic orientation and recoverability covenant**, not a metaphysical claim, empirical guarantee, or rule that retroactively declares every result desirable.

Its valid promise is:

> Whatever occurs may become material for learning, repair, redirection, rest, composting, or renewed action. No experience must be erased merely because it did not produce the intended result.

Its invalid expansion is:

> Every choice necessarily leads where the player truly wanted to go, every harm is secretly progress, or every blocker must be processed before the player may stop.

## Binding object model

### DestinationClaim

A **DestinationClaim** records a desired Point B, who authored it, its scope, time, revision history, and whether it remains active. A system may not silently revise Point B to make an outcome appear successful.

### RouteHypothesis

A **RouteHypothesis** proposes how actions, quests, capabilities, relationships, or transformations might change the possibility of reaching a DestinationClaim. It remains falsifiable.

### DivergenceRecord

A **DivergenceRecord** records departure from an expected route. Divergence may become discovery, evolution, error, failure, refusal, escape, repair, or noise. Which interpretation applies requires evidence and cannot be fixed by the metaphor alone.

### BlockerRecord

A **BlockerRecord** identifies something that constrains a specific route, action, capacity, or destination under declared conditions. It does not establish that the blocker is internal, emotional, caused by misaligned intention, or available for safe metabolization.

### RecoverabilityPath

A **RecoverabilityPath** is a presently available way to respond to an outcome: learn, retry, repair, request help, change method, change conditions, revise the destination, rest, route around, leave unresolved, abandon, or compost.

Recoverability is contextual. The system should not promise reversibility when harm, loss, disclosure, or external consequences cannot be undone.

## Blocker ruling

Some blockers may remain unresolved indefinitely.

For a specific route, an unresolved blocker may continue to constrain progress. This does not create an obligation to metabolize it. A player may instead:

- rest or pause;
- seek assistance or accommodation;
- alter the environment;
- choose another route;
- revise or release the destination;
- establish a boundary;
- accept a cost or limitation;
- preserve the blocker as unresolved data;
- abandon or compost the undertaking.

“There is no shortcut past the blockers” is valid only as a scoped causal statement: an operative constraint cannot be wished away while still claiming the same route succeeded under the same conditions. It does not mean every constraint is emotional, every obstacle must be transformed, or the original destination must remain binding.

## Operational meaning of “wrong”

The ontology distinguishes:

- **ethical wrong:** violation, coercion, avoidable harm, or breach;
- **instrumental error:** a move that fails its purpose;
- **predictive error:** an expectation contradicted by evidence;
- **route mismatch:** a path that does not support the active destination;
- **developmental information:** evidence that changes future possibility;
- **waste:** experience from which no current use is found;
- **unrecoverable consequence:** an effect that cannot be undone, even if learning follows.

The Yellow Brick Road may reframe error or waste as potentially usable material. It may not deny ethical wrong, failed purpose, or irreversible consequence.

## Player and system authority

1. The player or authorized collective authors and revises the destination.
2. The system may propose routes and recoverability paths.
3. Evidence determines whether a route advanced the declared destination.
4. The player may find personal meaning in divergence without being required to call it growth.
5. The system may preserve hope without falsifying the record.
6. In allyship, the person affected retains authority over their experience of help, consent, and harm.
7. Reauthoring Point B creates a revised DestinationClaim; it does not retroactively fulfill the prior one.

## Player-facing formulation

Preferred:

> **No path has to be wasted.** A wrong turn can remain wrong and still become information, story, repair, or the beginning of another road.

Mythic formulation, when the surrounding design preserves the distinctions above:

> **The road can continue from here.**

Avoid as an unqualified system guarantee:

> You can never go the wrong way.

## Hard invariants

1. Recoverability does not erase harm.
2. Learning from an error does not make the error necessary or correct.
3. Divergence does not prove hidden desire.
4. A revised destination does not retroactively fulfill the previous destination.
5. An unresolved blocker does not create perpetual processing obligation.
6. Refusal to metabolize is not itself a blocker diagnosis.
7. A route-around may be valid even when the original blocker remains.
8. A required dependency may still prevent completion of a particular route or quest.
9. Mythic reassurance cannot override falsifiable evidence.
10. The possibility of future meaning does not require finding meaning now.

## Resolution of the owner-correction question

“You can never go the wrong way” is retained as **mythic reassurance about recoverability and continued authorship**, not as a metaphysical claim or operational guarantee. The precise ontology rule is **no path has to be wasted**. Some blockers may remain unresolved indefinitely; what they constrain must be named, but the player is not obligated to process them.
